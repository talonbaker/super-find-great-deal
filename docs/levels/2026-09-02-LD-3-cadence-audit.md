# LD-3 — the cadence audit: where the "spread" lives, in seconds

Talon, 2026-08-29: *"everything is a bit too spread apart."* `docs/levels/2026-08-29-LEVEL-4-section-spacing.md`
(LEVEL-4) measured that the six bubbletest sections tile edge to edge with 5–10 m connector
seams — the spread is not between sections, it is inside them. This audit measures *where*,
region by region, along the line a player is meant to walk.

This is measurement only. It builds nothing — no geometry, no `.tscn`, no `.cs`. Findings
route to the next environment packet (A2-R2, the ring) per the LD-3 packet's scope.

## Method

**The tool.** `tools/dev/ld3_cadence.py` (515 lines) computes every number in this document
from three sources, none of them retyped:

- **Waypoint positions** — a node path in a section `.tscn`, resolved through
  `tools/dev/ld3_tscn.py` at the anchor `BubbleTest.tscn` actually instances it (not a hand-typed
  table — a moved anchor moves every number here with it); a prop in `BubbleTest.tscn`; or a
  constant read out of `scripts/game/world/bubbletest/BubbleTestLayout.cs` by regex.
- **Speeds** — read out of `scripts/net/MotorTuning.cs`'s `MotorTuning.Default`: jog (`MoveSpeed`)
  3.80 m/s, sprint (`MoveSpeed × SprintMultiplier`) 6.08 m/s. These match
  `docs/levels/METRICS-CARD.md` (generated by LD-1 from the same motor, 2026-09-02) exactly,
  which is corroboration, not a second source — both read `MotorTuning.Default`.
- **The lines** — *which* features a player meets in *which* order along a section's intended
  route is judgment, not derivation, and is written out in the tool as ordered waypoint lists
  (`LINES`, ~40 routes) so the judgment is inspectable and diffable against the code.

`python tools/dev/ld3_cadence.py --check` verifies every waypoint resolves against the current
scene files before any table is trusted; it passed clean after the merge below (`[ld3-cadence]
every waypoint resolves`). `python tools/dev/ld3_cadence.py` emits the tables below to stdout,
unedited — this document does not retype a single number out of it.

**Region types and thresholds — a value fork this packet owns** (research A2 synthesis 1: cadence
is the unit, author to seconds-between-features at arrival speed):

| Region | Threshold | Speed | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fast` | ≤ 3.0 s | sprint | open ground meant to be crossed at speed |
| `approach` | ≤ 5.0 s | sprint | the way to a thing — the hub plaza, bare plates, seams |
| `climb` | ≤ 5.0 s | jog | a stair or a spiral; arrival speed is a jog at best |
| — cap — | 8.0 s | region's own speed | flagged everywhere, regardless of region |

A gap over its region's threshold is `FLAG`; over 8 s anywhere is also `CAP`. A stair is reported
as **one row** with per-step statistics (Red's 23 treads, Blue's 37 pads, the tangle's 23 + 41
treads) — 120 rows of 0.4 s would bury the three gaps that matter, per the packet.

**Both directions.** Every line is read outbound and inbound. Where the return route is the
same waypoints reversed the gaps are identical and the table says so once (`"same gaps, read
the other way"`); where a summit is left by a drop instead of the stair (Red, Blue, Tangle) the
inbound line is authored separately and the fall is reported as run-to-lip metres plus free-fall
time under the shipped gravity (`MotorTuning.Default.Gravity × FallGravityMultiplier`), never
flagged — the player does nothing during the fall, so it is not a cadence gap.

**What only the live readout can confirm.** This is a desk audit: straight-line metres divided
by a nominal speed, not a played path. It cannot see a player who does not walk the intended
line, who stops to fight the camera on a stair, or who is slower than nominal because of a missed
jump. LD-2's live since-act readout (parallel packet, `CadenceTracker.cs` /
`CadenceClockTests.cs`, merged to master 2026-09-02) is the only source that can confirm these
numbers against actual play; this document cites it as "live confirmation available, not yet
run" rather than claiming it.

## Findings, ripe

**156 gaps measured, 23 flagged, 4 over the 8 s cap.** Every section but Red and Tangle's two
scored lines (main cairn, spur, far corner) carries at least one flagged gap; the flags cluster
in `approach` region on the connector plates and in Cyan's open lanes, not on the climbs
themselves — the stairs, spiral and stones are paced tightly by construction (no stair row is
flagged in either direction). That is itself a finding: **the spread Talon named is on the
ground between features, not on the way up.**

### 1. Cyan's middle aisle — 96.0 m / 15.8 s at sprint, the single worst gap in the level, and possibly not a defect

`Bubble_Seam_05` (0, 1.1, 45) to `Bubble_Cyan_13` (0, 1.1, 141) — the full length of the Cyan
section down the x = 0 centreline — has nothing on it. The line's own note, written into
`tools/dev/ld3_cadence.py` before this audit ran it, says *"the 12 m aisle is EMPTY by design
(program section 6.12 sightline)"*, citing `docs/agents/2026-08-27-bubble-test-program.md` §6
item 12: *"a sightline from the hub to the cyan run's far end so players can watch a friend drop
out of voice range."*

Read that citation and the note disagree with each other. §6.12 asks for a sightline to the
**far end** — the full ~100 m run, which is what is actually built — not a 12 m gap; "12 m" in
the note has no source I could find and does not match the measured 96 m. Two readings follow,
and this packet does not pick between them:

- **If the aisle is deliberately long** (the §6.12 reading), it is not a cadence defect at all —
  it is a second design goal (a voice-range sightline) that the `fast` region's blanket ≤ 3 s
  threshold was never written to accommodate, and the fix is not filler, it is a region type this
  tool does not have yet (a `sightline` region, unflagged by construction, distinct from `fast`).
- **If "12 m" was the real intent** and the aisle over-shot it eightfold, this is the worst
  cadence gap in the level and the cheapest fix is exactly what §6.12 does not want: filler
  breaks the sightline it exists to test.
- **Cheapest fix either way**, without touching the sightline: nothing *on* the x = 0 line, but a
  cadence read *off* it — Cyan's existing east/west lanes (the ruler lane, the slalom, the
  obstacle field) already flank this centreline within 15–30 m and are not flagged. A player who
  drifts off dead-centre by even a few metres is back inside threshold. What playing it would
  answer: whether players actually run the bare centreline (in which case the sightline reading
  needs a name and an exemption) or drift into the flanking lanes on their own (in which case
  there is no defect to fix).

### 2. Cyan's west obstacle field — 59.9 m / 9.9 s at sprint, from the seam to the first obstacle

`Bubble_Seam_05` (0, 1.1, 45) to `Obs_00 box + Cyan_16` (−58, 1.25, 60) is a real, unqualified
`fast`-region flag with no sightline rationale attached — nothing marks this stretch as
deliberate. **Cheapest fix:** one filler bubble roughly on the diagonal (around (−29, 1.2, 52),
the straight-line midpoint) brings both halves under 5 s at sprint; the obstacle field itself
(Obs_00 through Obs_09) is unflagged once entered. What playing it would answer: whether the
run-in from the seam reads as "warm-up before the field starts" (fine) or "empty ground" (not).

### 3. Green's north knoll — 42.8 m / 7.0 s at sprint, `approach` (threshold 5.0 s)

`Bubble_Green_12` (the greeting ridge) to `Bubble_Green_16` (half-sunk, north shore) is the
worst gap outside Cyan and the only flagged gap on Green's north branch (the west branch, to the
stepping stones, is clean). **Cheapest fix:** one bubble or a small terrain tell near
(−70, 0, 5) — roughly the segment's midpoint — splits it into two ≤ 4 s legs. What playing it
would answer: whether the north knoll reads as an intentional dead-end payoff (survivable at
7 s, since it is a branch rather than a through-route) or as the section running out of content
early — GreenHills is the smallest scored section and this is its only branch besides the
stones.

**A close fourth, not counted above:** Blue's seam-to-front-door approach (`Bubble_Seam_03` to
`MesaRamp1`, 42.4 m / 7.0 s) ties Green's north knoll within 0.4 m. Unlike Green's, this one sits
on the spine to a weenie (BlueTv, the reward at the top) rather than on a branch, which the
weenie-chain document's chain logic (§A4) makes the higher-priority of the two to fix first if
only one gets picked up.
