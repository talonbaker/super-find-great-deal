## 1. Why nothing could fall over, and the three signals that fixed it

FEEL-1 found the cause and left it here in as many words: **a prop at rest is `Freeze = true,
FreezeMode = Kinematic` on every peer**, so a held crate driven into a row of boxes is stopped by
an immovable wall of them. Its suite's `fastest never-held prop 0.00 m/s` against a 3 m/s bar was
not evidence the cap worked — nothing moved at all.

P1 says to reuse the contact signal rather than add a monitor. That turns out to be possible for
**all three** movers, because each already has one, and the interesting part is that they are
three *different* signals for three different reasons:

| mover | signal | why not `body_entered` |
|---|---|---|
| a **held** prop | FEEL-1's per-tick `PhysicsServer3D.BodyTestMotion` sweep, which already knows what stopped the crate at the shelf | both bodies are frozen kinematic, and Godot reports such a pair **nothing at all** — SFX-1 measured exactly this one system over ("only the FALLING can got a `body_entered`") |
| a **loose** prop | `Carryable.body_entered`, read a second time | — |
| an **avatar** | `AvatarMotor`'s existing bump scan, which now also reports *which* collider | a `CharacterBody3D` is not a rigid body; its contacts are slide collisions |

**The held case costs zero new queries** on the holder's peer: the sweep result was already being
computed and its collider was being thrown away. The server's copy of a **client**-held prop has
no sweep of its own (`StepFollowHolder` writes the pose directly), so that arm gains one motion
query per client-held prop per tick — at most one per player. Without it, a client could carry a
crate through a stack and only the host would ever see it fall, which is DOOR-1's defect ("the
server's log said *shoved 3 prop(s)* and it was telling the truth") wearing a new hat.

**The loose case needed the gates unpicking, and this is the subtle half.** `OnBodyEntered` early-
outs on `IsHeld`, on a 0.4 s per-body cooldown, and below a 2 m/s audibility floor. All three are
right for sound and wrong for physics:

- the **cooldown** would swallow the second can of a collapsing stack, which is inside the first's;
- the **2 m/s floor** would silently refuse a can rolled into a pyramid at 1 m/s — inaudible, and
  it must still bring the pyramid down;
- `IsHeld` is the domino case itself.

So the wake is reported from **above** all three, with its own far lower gate
(`PropPhysics.WakeSpeedThresholdMps`, 0.35 m/s — a seventh of the audibility floor).

**The avatar case is replay-safe by construction and that took a decision.** `AvatarMotor.Step` is
run by client prediction and again by server reconciliation. The collider goes in `StepEvents`,
which is what a step *reports*, not in `MoveState`, which is what a rewind *replays*; and the wake
is consumed on the server's authoritative step and on a host's owner step, **never in
`Reconcile`'s replay loop**. A rewind must not knock the same stack down twice.

**Every decision past "was there a contact" is in one place** — `PropManager.ServerBumpProp`, on
the server, in the class that already owns prop authority. The physical body reports and knows
nothing else, which is `ImpactReporter`'s own architecture applied a second time.
`PropRegistry.Wake` is DOOR-1's `Resting -> Loose` edge, reused unchanged: it refuses a *held*
prop for the reason its own doc gives, so that case needed no new guard.

## 2. Bounded energy, and the one place I departed from P2's letter

**The real guarantee is at the impulse.** `PropPhysics.WakeSpeed` clamps every wake to the bar, so
nothing this build *does* to a prop can hand it more than 3 m/s — before any per-tick check runs.
The model is the one-dimensional inelastic collision, `(1 + e)·m·v / (m + M)` with `e = 0.2`, and
**the mover's own mass with no allowance for the arm behind a held one**: a held crate is driven by
a spring whose step is already speed-capped, and crediting the holder's body mass would let a 2.4
m/s walk deliver the full bar to everything it brushed. At the reference numbers — a 1 kg crate at
the browse pace into a 0.4 kg cereal box — that is **2.06 m/s**, which topples the box and leaves a
third of the bar unspent.

### The departure, stated rather than smuggled

P2 reads literally as "no prop may exceed 3 m/s after any interaction that is not a throw". **A
prop knocked off a 1.5 m shelf is doing 5.4 m/s by the time it lands.** Clamping that would play
every fall in slow motion — a "fix" a player reads as the physics being broken, which is the
defect this packet exists to remove rather than to add.

So the bar is applied to **what an interaction writes** and not to what gravity owns:

| component | ceiling |
|---|---|
| horizontal | `MaxPropSpeedMps` (3.0) — or a throw's own cap while its launch energy is unspent |
| upward | `MaxPropSpeedMps` — nothing in a supermarket launches itself |
| downward | `MaxPropFallSpeedMps` (12.0), far above any indoor drop, there only so nothing can tunnel out between two ticks |

`PropPhysicsTests.GravityIsNotAnInteraction_AFallingPropIsNotSlowedToTheBar` pins it.

A **throw** keeps `ThrowSpeedCapMps` (8.5, covering CARRY-1's 7.5 forward + 3.2 up) until its
*horizontal* energy is spent once, then latches down and never rises again for that episode. The
allowance is granted by the **verb** at the release funnel, not inferred from a number: a prop
judged only on its speed would be clamped in the first tick of every throw. Reading the horizontal
component rather than the whole speed is what stops a throw that is mostly falling from latching
early mid-arc.

Angular velocity gets its own ceiling (`MaxPropSpinRadPerSec`, 40 rad/s — a can rolling at 1 m/s
on a 35 mm radius turns at 28, so this only ever catches a solver explosion). P2 names speed only;
the spin is the other half of "make other objects jump around randomly" and is stated here rather
than left.

Every clamp prints `[phys] clamp prop=N to X m/s (cap Y) total=Z` and increments
`PropManager.PropClampCount`. **The count is the claim**, and the suite gates on it being at most 2.

## 3. The rest audit waits, and what it deliberately does NOT gate

REACH-1's audit is itself one of the things that can make a prop jump. Its second correction step
returns a prop to its last-good transform, and **a prop snapping back to a ten-second-old pose half
a metre in front of the person who just nudged it is exactly the freakout Talon described** — with
every line of REACH-1 working as designed.

**Depenetration is untouched and unconditional.** Pushing a crate 4 cm out of a wall is the common
case, it keeps the prop where the player left it, and gating it would regress the defect REACH-1
was cut to fix. Only the **last-good restore** is gated, on both of P3's conditions:

```
stuck for >= PropRestGate.StuckHoldSec (1.0 s)   AND   no avatar's grab ray within
                                                        PropRestGate.PlayerAttentionM (2.0 m)
```

A refusal changes **nothing** — the prop stays exactly as it is, Loose if it was Loose — and logs
one line a second per prop:

```
[phys] rest-wait prop=N stuck=0.63s nearestGrabRay=0.41m (needs >=1.00s and >2.00m)
```

The settle latch brings it back in another `SettleTicks` (18) and it corrects itself the moment
either condition clears. Nothing is dropped and no timer of its own was added.

**Two details that are easy to get wrong and are pinned by tests.** "No avatars" must read as
**far**, never as zero — the seeker's whole round happens in a room the hider has left, which is
precisely where correcting is safe. And the grab ray is a **segment** from the eye out to
`GrabRange`, not an infinite line: a prop behind the player is on that line and is not something
they are looking at.

## 4. The material table

Authored, not coded, per P4. Friction and bounce are on four `PhysicsMaterial` resources — **one
per `PropMaterial` voice**, so a prop's sound and its feel come off one classification and cannot
disagree about what a thing is made of. The per-body quantities a `PhysicsMaterial` cannot carry
are native `RigidBody3D` properties in each prefab's `.tscn`; `Can.tscn`'s own header already
records that a native property survives nested-`PackedScene` instancing on this build where a C#
`[Export]` does not, and `physics_material_override`, `angular_damp`, `center_of_mass` and
`can_sleep` are all native.

| prop | voice | friction | bounce | linear damp | angular damp | centre of mass | sleeps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Can** (0.35 kg, r 35 mm × 120 mm) | tin | **0.25** | **0.10** | 0.30 | **0.25** | auto | **no** |
| **Cereal box** (0.4 kg, 190×280×60 mm) | cardboard | **0.75** | 0.00 | 0.40 | **1.20** | **+0.07 m (custom)** | yes |
| **Crate** (1.0 kg, 0.44 m cube) | wood | 0.60 | 0.00 | 0.30 | 1.00 | auto | yes |
| **Produce** (0.25 kg, r 80 mm) | produce | 0.90 `rough` | 0.00 | 2.00 (unchanged) | 3.00 (unchanged) | auto | yes |
| **Ball** (foundation, r 0.26 m) | wood | 0.60 | 0.00 | 2.00 (unchanged) | 2.00 (unchanged) | auto | yes |

The floor and the shelves author no material, so they are Godot's default 1.0 and the combined
figure at a contact is the prop's own — which is what makes the table read directly as "how this
object behaves on a board".

**The two rows that ARE Talon's sentence.** Tin's 0.25 friction with a 0.25 angular damp is why a
can rolls: at the box's 1.2 a nudged can turns a third of a revolution and stops dead, which reads
as the floor being made of tar. Cardboard's 0.75 is the opposite half — a domino needs its **base
held** while its top is pushed, and at the can's friction a shoved box skates along the board and
arrives at its neighbour still upright.

**The centre of mass is the other half of the domino, and it composes with the impulse's
application point.** The box's centre of mass is 0.07 m above the geometric centre — three quarters
of the way up — and `NetworkedProp.WakeFromContactServer` applies the impulse at the **contact
point**, not at the centre of mass. So a box struck high goes over and a box struck at its base
slides a little, which is what the player expects. A can gets no override: P4 says in as many words
that a can is not top-heavy, and one that were would tip instead of rolling.

**`can_sleep = false` on the can alone.** Godot's sleep thresholds are project-wide and tuned for
bodies that have arrived; a can put to sleep mid-roll freezes in front of the player. The cost is
nothing, because a prop is a live rigid body only while it is Loose.

**Deal variants are identical to the digit**, physics material and centre of mass included, and
`DealPrefabTests` now asserts the four new fields alongside mass and damping. SHELF-1's rule is
that a near-miss differs by the band alone — **a deal can that rolled further than its
forty-seven neighbours would be findable by watching it move**, which is the same defect as being
findable by silhouette.

### The settle latch now asks the spin as well as the speed

The linear gate alone latches a prop that is still visibly turning — a can crosses 0.2 m/s well
before it stops — and freezes it kinematic mid-roll on every peer. `PropManager.SettleSpinSq` is
the same question asked of the other degree of freedom, **derived rather than picked**: a 35 mm can
rolling at the linear settle speed turns at 0.2/0.035 = 5.71 rad/s, so the two gates close at the
same physical moment. `SettleSpeedSq` is now written as the square of a named speed so the two
cannot drift apart by hand (the value moves by 3e-9).

## 5. P6 needed nothing, and P7 cost no wire

**P6.** `Carryable._Ready` has set `ContinuousCd = true` **unconditionally** since the foundation,
with the comment that says why ("a thrown prop can otherwise tunnel through a thin wall/door in one
physics step"). That is strictly stronger than P6's "for Loose props above 1 m/s", so this packet
adds nothing and changes nothing. Stated rather than silently skipped.

**P7.** `PropRelease.Bumped` = **4**, appended. Nobody dropped, placed or threw a prop that was
knocked, so none of the three existing verbs describes it and reporting one would hand the sound
layer the wrong event for the only transition in the game with no holder at either end.
`PropReleaseWire.Decode`'s unknown-ordinal arm is what made spending the byte safe: no field was
added, no message widened, **`ProtocolVersion` unchanged at 16**. `PropImpactWireTests`'
unknown-ordinal theory moved from 4 to 5, which is the whole cost.

## 8. Deviations from the packet, each with its reason

1. **P2's clamp is on the interaction's components, not on the whole velocity** (§2). Clamping
   gravity would slow every fall to 3 m/s.
2. **An angular ceiling was added beside the linear one** (§2). "Jump around randomly" is spin as
   much as speed; P2 names only the speed.
3. **P5's 30-second stability is measured as a 1 cm bar over the run's own pre-contact window, and
   the weight is on the re-settle instead** (§6). A frozen kinematic body is stable by
   construction and proves nothing.
4. **P6 required no change** (§5). `ContinuousCd` is already unconditional, which is stronger.
5. **The can pyramid is a CAPTURE, not a suite bar.** `Run-PhysicsFeelTest`'s bars are an angle and
   a distance; a pyramid is a picture, and `Capture-PhysicsFeel.ps1` stages one.
6. **Bar (e) is stated as containment rather than as one board.** The suite asserts a thrown prop
   never leaves the room envelope or ends up under the floor, rather than naming a 5 cm board whose
   position this lane could not verify without the machine. Weaker than the packet's wording and
   said so.
