## 1b. THE BRANCH DOES NOT LOAD UNTIL IT IS BAKED

`SearchRoom.tscn` instances `StockBulk.tscn` and `FloorBin.tscn` instances `BinMound.tscn`, and
**neither generated file is committed until the bake has been run**, because the bake needs a
`dotnet build` and this lane's build/Godot window was held by the REVIEW-1 fix lane's marathon
for the whole of the design phase (the packet's Concurrency section, binding).

**Before anything in this branch will open or run:**

```
SFGD_BAKE_STOCK=1 dotnet test tests/unit/SailNet.Tests.csproj
```

That writes both scene files from `ShelfStock`/`StockRoom` and then every later `dotnet test`
compares them. Nothing merges anywhere until the gates in §5 are green, so this state never
reaches another lane — but a reader who checks the branch out before that will see a room
referencing two scenes that are not there, and this is why.

## 3. The design, and the three places it departs from the packet

### 3.1 BAKED, not built at scene load — the one that needed a ruling

**The packet asked for a `ShelfStocker` node that fills MultiMesh buffers in `_Ready`, seeded per
bay.** `.claude/rules/godot-scenes.md` says the opposite, unconditionally and with a date on it:

> **Every part of a level is authored in its scene file, never built in code** (Talon, 2026-08-27).

and `SupermarketWorldSelfTest`'s own class doc says what the rule is *for*:

> The standing rule ... is that every part of a level is physically authored in the scene file
> **so it can be opened and flown around in the editor**. That is not something a reviewer can
> verify from a diff, because a room that builds a shelf in `_Ready` looks identical in the
> inspector to one that does not. So it is measured.

**A `ShelfStocker` would have PASSED that measurement.** `CheckAuthored` counts NODES, packed
against live, and filling a MultiMesh buffer adds none — so the room would have gone green while
being exactly what the rule forbids: empty in the editor, full at runtime. Passing a check for
the wrong reason is worse than failing it.

**The packet's REASON is kept whole.** It asked for procedural generation because *"1500–3000
instances by hand is unreviewable"*, and that is right. So the same seeded arithmetic runs in a
generator, writes the scene files, and the result is committed — and
`StockBakeTests.TheBakedSceneFilesAreExactlyWhatTheArithmeticProduces` re-derives the entire
layout on every `dotnet test` and compares it byte for byte. The committed file is a pure
function of eighty lines of arithmetic; a reviewer reads the arithmetic; a hand edit to the
generated file is a red with a line number. It is also strictly cheaper: zero load-time cost on
every peer instead of ~2 700 instances' worth each.

**Generalisation, and it is in `.claude/rules/test-suite.md` now:** a node-count check cannot see
work that goes into a RESOURCE rather than into a node, so "it passes the level check" is not
evidence that a level is authored.

### 3.2 End-caps carry PRODUCE, not stacked box displays

The packet gives the end-caps "stacked box displays (pallet stacks)". SHELF-1 had already
authored **twelve carryable oranges onto those same shelves** (two per board, six per end-cap,
ids 1106..1117), and bulk's whole contract is that it is the same product as the facing in front
of it — SFX-1 gave each material its own voice, so a cardboard thud out of a shelf of oranges is
a lie a player can hear at the exact moment they are trying to work out where the other player
is. The stacked box displays are the two **floor pallets**, which is what a stacked display
physically is; an end-cap is a shelf.

### 3.3 The bin mounds have no collision

The packet asks for a pile collider "so a placed object nestles on the mound rather than floating
or sinking". SHELF-1's bible check closed INTERACTION §3 on this exact object:

> a bin is an open-top tub with a real hollow: 0.78 × 0.54 × 0.78 interior, which **clears a
> 0.44 m crate by 0.17 m horizontally and 0.10 m vertically**, so "hide it under the bin" is a
> physical fact and not a scripted allowance.

The interior is 0.54 m tall and a crate needs 0.44 m of it. **Any mound collider deletes that
affordance outright — there is no half-fill that keeps it.** Visual-only keeps both: the hollow
is still hollow to physics, and an object dropped into a stocked bin settles on the bin floor
*among* the oranges, which is both what really happens and a better hiding place than an empty
tub. The cost is stated rather than hidden: something set down there beds into the fruit instead
of resting on top of it.

`BinMound.tscn` is a child of `FloorBin.tscn`, not of the room, for a reason that would otherwise
have been a level bug: a bin is a 6 kg `RigidBody3D` a player can pick up and tip over, and
static oranges authored at a bin's room position would hang in the air the first time anybody
moved one.

### 3.4 Nothing is stacked two high on a board, and the packet's own words cover it

"Boxes 2 deep and stacked 2 high **where the board clearance allows**." The clearance is 0.50 m
and two cereal boxes are 0.56 m, so the conditional is false on every board. Stacks live on the
floor pallets, where 1.40 m of them fits under the bays' 1.80 m skyline — the line SHELF-1 chose
so a bay hides the aisle behind it from a standing player.

### 3.5 The bulk mesh is a coarser tessellation of the prefab's mesh

`CylinderMesh` defaults to 64 radial segments and `SphereMesh` to 64×32; at those counts the bulk
cans alone are about a million triangles. Every dimension that can be seen or collided with is
identical to the prefab's — radius, height, albedo, roughness, metallic, emission, all asserted
against the prefab by `ShelfStockTests` — and the segment count is the one number a player cannot
tell apart on a 70 mm object at half a metre. It is an LOD, not different art.

## 4. The layout, as arithmetic

Everything below is `scripts/game/world/stock/ShelfStock.cs` and `StockRoom.cs`, engine-free and
`dotnet test`-able in milliseconds. The room's own geometry is **read out of `SearchRoom.tscn`**
(by the generator and by `dotnet test`) and **out of the live tree** (by the engine self-test),
never tabulated — so the layout has exactly one source of truth and moving a bay in the editor
moves its stock with it.

### 4.1 The grid

| | cans | cereal boxes | produce |
|---|---|---|---|
| product | 0.07 × 0.12 × 0.07 | 0.19 × 0.28 × 0.06 | r 0.08 |
| slot pitch along the bay | 0.09 m | 0.21 m | 0.175 m |
| slots across a 2.18 m clear span | **24** | **10** | — |
| slots across a 1.28 m end-cap | — | 6 | **7** |
| depth rows × pitch | 4 × 0.10 | 4 × 0.10 | 2 × 0.20 |
| rows on boards 0 and 1 | all four | all four | both |
| rows on board 2 | **the two outer only** | the two outer only | the outer one |

**Board 2 carries its two outer rows and nothing between them, and that is a sightline decision.**
REACH-1 measured the avatar's eye at **1.040 m**; board 2's surface is **1.525 m**. From anywhere
a player can stand you see that shelf's front edge and the undersides, and its middle rows are
geometrically invisible from both sides. A full facing at each of the bay's two open faces is
what the player actually sees, and it is half the top shelf's instance cost.

### 4.2 The holes

One to three per board, seeded from the bay's node NAME (a node name is native and survives
instancing — `.claude/rules/godot-scenes.md` has paid four times for identity carried on an
`[Export]`). The first hole on every board goes all the way through, so every bay keeps a
sightline a player can see and shove a can through — SHELF-1's "root around" property, which is
what keeps this room somewhere to search rather than four walls with tins on them.

**The rule that is a GAMEPLAY rule and not a layout rule: every hole is cleared from a FACE
inward, never out of the middle.** REACH-1's layer 3 refuses a placement whose only sightline is
blocked by STATIC geometry (`ReachReason.OccludedByStatic`), and bulk is static. A hole with
filler still in front of it is a hiding place the hider can use and the game will then refuse the
Confirm on — *the round ends in a shrug, which is the worst outcome this design has.* Asserted
from the emitted layout, not from the code that emits it
(`ShelfStockTests.EveryHoleReachesAFaceWithNoBulkInFrontOfIt`).

A hole is **3 can-slots (0.27 m)** or **2 box/produce-slots**, and every one of them clears the
widest object a hider can be carrying at any yaw — the cereal box's 0.199 m plan diagonal — by
more than twice `PlacementIntegrity.DefaultOverlapToleranceM` on each side.

### 4.3 Collision

Static, layer 1 (the mask `PlacementIntegrity.QueryMask` reads), **sized to the filled volume per
slot-run**. Two adjacent slots merge into one box only when their depth masks are identical, so a
shallow hole's slots get their own shorter box rather than being swallowed by the run beside them.
One box per board would have made every hole in the room a wall that looks like a hole.

### 4.4 The carve-out, which is the defect that would have broken the room

**SHELF-1's 120 products are networked `RigidBody3D` standing on the same boards the filler
fills.** Static filler laid over one puts a networked prop permanently inside static geometry:
every rest audit reports `StaticOverlap`, REACH-1's layer 3 answers `InsideStatic`, and a hider
who chose that prop is refused the Confirm with `NobodyCouldReachThat` — **in a room that renders
perfectly and whose every other suite stays green.** A hundred and twenty of those is the whole
room broken, quietly.

So every cell whose product box would come within **10 mm** of an authored facing is emptied, and
the facing ends up standing in its own hole in the filler, which is what a front row *is*.
`StockBakeTests.NoBulkInstanceStandsWhereACarryableFacingStands` is the gate.

**Why 10 mm and not the 0.02 m placement tolerance, measured rather than chosen:** at produce's
0.175 m pitch for a 0.16 m sphere, a 0.02 m clearance empties both NEIGHBOURS of every facing as
well and leaves an end-cap board with 2 of 14 cells standing — barer than SHELF-1 left it. The
quantity that matters is PENETRATION, and any positive clearance is already zero penetration.

A hole may also only be cut in **intact** stock (every slot it spans still carrying its board's
full depth), with a deterministic scan behind the random draws, so "every board keeps a hole" is
a claim about the geometry rather than about the luck of a seed.
