## 1. The recipes, with ordinals

Eleven new `Sfx` members. **Twelve table cells, eleven members**, and both halves of that
arithmetic are deliberate: the packet's Thrown row is *"one shared brief noise sweep"* for all
three materials, so `Whoosh` is one member serving three cells; `TinBuzz` is the twelfth, an
Impact **layer** rather than an Impact sound (see the note under the table).

`// 24 = Bang (DOOR-1)` sits in the enum where 24 would be, so that merge is a no-op.

| Ordinal | Member | Event | Duration | Synthesis, in one line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | `TinPick` | PickedUp | 0.15 s | 1850 Hz with inharmonic partials at **1 : 2.76 : 5.40**, near-instant attack; the upper two carry extra decay so the clink thins as it fades instead of holding a chord |
| 26 | `TinClank` | Impact | 0.40 s | the same shell modes at 620 Hz so the body of the can reads rather than its rim, ringing the full 0.4 s, with a ~20 ms band-limited noise burst welded to the front — the strike before the shell |
| 27 | `TinBuzz` | Impact **layer** | 0.25 s | 240 Hz two-harmonic tone hard amplitude-modulated at 62 Hz: a thin wall hit harder than it can absorb |
| 28 | `TinTick` | Placed | 0.04 s | rim: the same ratios at 3200 Hz, two partials, steep decay — a tick, not a chime |
| 29 | `CardPick` | PickedUp | 0.20 s | band-passed noise and **nothing else**, 20 ms attack, slow double-sine grain on the amplitude (contents shifting). A faster attack reads as a click |
| 30 | `CardThud` | Impact | 0.27 s | hollow "bop": 128 Hz sagging a third of its pitch over 0.12 s plus a low noise puff, then **0.15 s of decaying rustle**. Never rings |
| 31 | `CardSettle` | Placed | 0.08 s | the rustle again, steeper, with a barely-there 110 Hz bump so the box has weight without having a note |
| 32 | `ProducePick` | PickedUp | 0.06 s | one damped 180 Hz sine sagging slightly, and nothing else — almost a pat |
| 33 | `ProduceThump` | Impact | 0.10 s | 95 Hz damped sine with a 30 ms heavily-filtered noise front. No ring at all: the flesh absorbs it |
| 34 | `ProducePlop` | Placed | 0.07 s | a sine whose pitch **falls fast** (260 → 120 Hz) rather than sags — the falling pitch *is* the plop |
| 35 | `Whoosh` | Thrown, all three | 0.12 s | noise whose band sweeps open and shut (the low-pass coefficient IS the sweep), swelling in and out with no attack transient and no tail |

Also appended: **`ActorEvent.Placed = 14`**. CARRY-1 split place from drop in the gameplay layer
and left the presentation layer unable to tell them apart; a can set on a shelf is a 40 ms rim
tick and a can tossed on the floor is not.

**`TinBuzz` is a second response on the same event, not a second recipe chosen in code.** A baked
one-shot cannot vary its own timbre with hit speed — the PCM is rendered once and cached forever
— so *"at high intensity add a buzz layer"* is expressed the way this presentation system already
expresses conditional response: the tin profile maps Impact to **both** `TinClank` (always) and
`TinBuzz` (`MinIntensity 0.6`), and the existing intensity gate in `ActorFx.FireCore` decides.
Zero new code paths, and Talon retunes the threshold in the `.tres`. Observed firing correctly in
the smoke at intensity 0.872.

### What makes a material legible, since it is three things and the recipes spend everything on them

1. **Partial ratios.** A struck tin can is a thin shell and a shell's modes are *inharmonic*. All
   three tin recipes use 1 : 2.76 : 5.40, the circular-plate ratios, and that is the entire
   reason they read as metal rather than as a pitched beep. Cardboard and produce use no partial
   stack at all — a box and an apple have no modes that survive long enough to hear.
2. **Decay.** Tin rings 0.4 s, and its upper partials decay *faster* than the fundamental, which
   is what makes a ring sound like a ring rather than a chord. Cardboard and produce do not ring
   at any length.
3. **Noise character.** Cardboard's signature is band-passed noise with a slow amplitude grain.
   Tin's noise is a 20 ms burst at the very front only. Produce's is 30 ms of soft low noise.

Every recipe seeds its own `System.Random` with a fixed literal, so the baked buffer is
byte-identical on every machine and every run — which is what makes the `.wav` renders a stable
artefact rather than churn, and what lets the unit suite assert a peak amplitude rather than only
a range.

## 2. How a scene sets the material, and why the export is not the answer

**SHELF-1: instance the prefab. Do not write `material =` on the instance line, and do not expect
the one inside the prefab to arrive.**

`PropManager.AuthoredKindOf` already recorded the trap and SFX-1 inherited it: **this project's
Godot/Mono build does not apply a nested PackedScene instance's own exported script properties.**
`Crate.tscn`'s authored `kind` and `tint` both read back as the C# field's default on every
authored prop. A level authors a prop as

```
[node name="Prop_0" type="Node3D" parent="."]
script = ExtResource("netprop")
[node name="Body" parent="Prop_0" instance=ExtResource("Can.tscn")]
```

— which is exactly that shape. So an `[Export] PropMaterial` alone would be silently ignored for
every prop a level designer ever places, and the whole packet would work only for code-built
fixtures.

**So the resolution rule has a fallback that provably survives instancing: the collider.**

```
explicit Profile export   ->  wins outright (a prop that needs its own voice, not its material's)
Material export != Wood   ->  the code-built path, and the authored path on any build that applies it
collider shape            ->  the authored path on THIS build          <- the one that actually fires
Wood                      ->  the shared default profile, unchanged
```

`Carryable.ShapeFromCollider` is the one table, and `PropManager.AuthoredKindOf` now delegates to
it, so the sound layer and the authority layer can never disagree about what a prop is:

| Collider | Shape | Discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| `CylinderShape3D` | `Can` | free — nothing else is a cylinder |
| `BoxShape3D` | `Crate` if cubic, else `Box` | **"are all three extents equal"** — a crate is 0.44³ and a cereal box is emphatically not. A meaningful question, not a magic number |
| `SphereShape3D` | `Produce` if r ≤ 0.17, else `Ball` | the one genuinely arbitrary number here, and it is the midpoint of the two authored radii (0.08 and 0.26) rather than hugging either. Named `Carryable.ProduceVsBallRadiusM` |

**The escape hatch for a shape that breaks the table** — a future cubic cardboard box, say — is
the explicit `Profile` export, which is why it wins over the collider rather than the other way
round.

The exports are still written into all three prefabs. They are true, they are documentation, and
they apply on the code-built path; nothing depends on them.

## 3. The four ways a sound was inaudible, all found by the smoke

Every one of these was found by `tests/Run-MaterialSfxTest.ps1` going red, none was predicted,
and each produced a **partly** working feature — which is the kind that ships.

### 3.1 `LinearVelocity` inside `body_entered` is not the impact speed

Measured, and the failure is one-sided, which is what makes it easy to miss:

```
[sfx] sfx TinClank  event=Impact intensity=0.895 at (43.41,1.09,-4.95) src=1   <- thrown at a WALL
[sfx] sfx CardThud  event=Impact intensity=0.875 at (41.98,1.33,-4.94) src=2   <- thrown at a WALL
   ... and four props dropped 0.6-1.1 m onto the FLOOR: nothing, every run
```

A glancing contact leaves residual velocity for the handler to read. A head-on landing has
already had its normal impulse applied by the time the signal is emitted, so the handler reads a
body that has stopped. **`Carryable.ApproachSpeedMps` samples the speed going INTO the physics
step** (top of `_PhysicsProcess`, which Godot calls before the physics server integrates), and
`SpeedOf` takes the largest of live velocity, approach speed and observed speed. All three
materials fire now.

### 3.2 An impact was audible to the host and to nobody else

A Loose prop on a non-authority peer is a **frozen kinematic body** lerped toward a streamed
transform, so its `LinearVelocity` is 0 forever and the speed gate rejected every contact.
`Carryable.ObservedSpeedMps`, written each tick by `NetworkedProp` while it follows the stream,
gives that body a speed. **This predates SFX-1** — `Thunk` had it too.

**It is not enough, and the suite says so every run rather than hiding it.** Godot reports a
frozen kinematic body no contact at all, so the remote peer still hears no impact:

```
REMOTE PEER (not the host): 9 prop sound(s) - PickedUp 3, Thrown 6, Impact 0
NOTE: the remote peer heard no IMPACT. ... closing it needs one bit on the wire.
```

**This is the biggest thing left open and it is in §6.**

### 3.3 A throw was announced where only the host could hear it

`Carryable.OnThrown` is reached only from `NetworkedProp.BeginLooseServer`, which is server-only.
The fire moved to **`NetworkedProp.BeginLoose`** — the every-peer half of Held → Loose, reached
through `ApplyPropState`, which is `CallLocal`. The remote peer now hears all seven releases.

**A PLACE arrives there too and a peer cannot tell it from a throw.** That is a wire fact, not a
shortcut: CARRY-1's own handoff records that *"at ApplyPropState a place and a drop are the same
Held→Loose transition and are indistinguishable there"*. So every release plays the shared
`Whoosh`, which is the one sound in this palette that is a fact about the **arm** rather than
about the object — the least wrong thing to play when the verb is unknown. The deliberate
set-down tick (`Placed`) stays on `PlaceLooseServer`, where the verb IS known, and is therefore
host-only today.

### 3.4 The once-per-contact rule could be silent rather than single

The packet offered "the lower instance id, or the faster one — pick one rule, state it". **Both
are wrong, and the reason is worth more than the rule.**

Instance id is a total order, so it looked strictly better than speed (which ties when a whole
shelf is in the same free fall, and a tie means both fire, which is the bug). It has one fatal
property: **it picks the winner before knowing whether the winner will ever be asked.** Measured
— a can dropped onto a can that had already settled produced **no sound at all**, run after run.
Only the falling can got a `body_entered`; the resting one is frozen kinematic and Godot never
asked it anything; and it happened to hold the higher instance id because it was seeded second.
The rule silenced the only body in a position to speak.

**`Carryable.ClaimPropOnPropContact` is first-come**: the first call for a pair inside a 60 ms
window fires and records the claim, the second is suppressed. Whoever is asked, fires. It costs a
small table (opportunistically pruned) and a clock, and it degrades in the right direction — the
worst case is one extra sound, not a swallowed one.

**Generalises, and it is in the rules file: a de-duplication rule that decides its winner up
front is silent whenever only the loser is asked.**

## 4. The intensity mapping

- **Relative contact speed**, over `[ThunkSpeedThreshold 2.0, ImpactSpeedCeiling 8.0]` m/s,
  clamped at both ends. Relative rather than own, because a can standing on a shelf that a thrown
  box slams into is stationary — its own speed is zero and it would have been silent while being
  knocked across the room.
- **Volume**: `VolumeDb + IntensityVolumeBoostDb × clamp(i)`, plus the caller's listener trim,
  which is deliberately a different dial (`ActorFx.Fire`'s own doc has the reasoning).
- **Pitch**: a new `EventResponse.IntensityPitchRange`, `1 + range × clamp(i)`, applied *under*
  the random jitter and floored at 0.25. **The sign is the legible part**: tin is **+0.18** and
  cardboard/produce are **−0.14 / −0.12**. A thin shell struck harder excites its higher modes;
  a panel or a piece of flesh struck harder moves more mass. Zero by default, so every response
  authored before this field plays at exactly the pitch it always did.
- The 0.4 s per-body cooldown is untouched.
- Everything positional through `SfxLab.PlayStream3D`. No new bus.

**The holder's own soft `Bump` on a scrape** (the packet's "implement only if it falls out of the
same code path in under 20 lines") — **not implemented, noted as follow-up.** It does not fall
out: held props are gated by `IsHeld` *before* the contact handler does anything, and the holder's
scrape is a different event on a different actor's profile (the avatar's, not the prop's) with its
own threshold. It is a small packet, not a branch.

### The `.wav` renders

`docs/qa/2026-09-19-sfx-1/` — 11 files, one per material per event plus the shared throw:
`tin-pickedup`, `tin-impact`, `tin-impact-hard-layer`, `tin-placed`, `cardboard-pickedup`,
`cardboard-impact`, `cardboard-placed`, `produce-pickedup`, `produce-impact`, `produce-placed`,
`shared-thrown`. **16-bit PCM, 44.1 kHz, mono.**

They are written by the unit test that also asserts them, which is deliberate: a headless Godot
run has no audio device and a windowed one cannot be recorded by a suite, so an offline render is
the only honest capture, and generating the artefact in the same place that validates it is what
stops the two drifting. Output is byte-deterministic, so a rerun leaves `git status` clean.

**One deviation:** the recipes render at the project's pinned 48 kHz and are linearly resampled
to the 44.1 kHz asked for. Linear rather than windowed-sinc on purpose — these are 40–400 ms
percussive clips for a human to listen to, the ratio is 0.92, and the alias energy is tens of dB
under material whose whole character is already a noise band. **Nothing in the game resamples**;
the engine plays the 48 kHz buffer directly.

## 6. Voice budget, and a bar that pool size cannot reach

Forty mixed props released together with two players walking through them — the shelf-collapse
event the aisles will produce, seeded in a heap because SHELF-1 has not landed and the packet
says not to wait:

| `PoolSize` | fires | `PeakLive3DVoices` | `OneShotSteals` | stolen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 (INT-0's) | 54 | **14** | 26 | **48 %** |
| **18 (now)** | **56** | **18** | **22** | **39 %** |

**The peak landing exactly ON the pool size both times is the tell** — the pool saturated, rather
than the mix happening to want that many.

**Raised to 18, and it is paid for rather than borrowed.** The ceiling was exactly spent
(5 + 14 + 5 = 24), so the four slots came off `SfxLab.LoopPoolSize` (**5 → 1**): its only member
is `SfxLoop.FireBody`, an **outdoor fire bed** from a game about a forest at night, which
`docs/PRUNE-BACKLOG.md` §1 already lists as dead and kept for build-green. There are no fires in
a supermarket. One loop slot is kept rather than zero so the partition's acquire/release
lifecycle and its refuse-rather-than-steal policy stay reachable instead of rotting.
`AudioVoiceBudget.Ceiling` is **untouched at 24** — 5 + 18 + 1.

**The packet's 10 % steal bar is not reachable by pool size at this fixture, and the suite says
so rather than chasing it.** Forty simultaneous impacts want forty voices; no pool that fits
under 24 serves them. The suite reports the design bar every run and gates on a 60 % regression
bar, well clear of the measured 39 %. **The fix is source-side limiting** — the same answer
`FootstepAudioDirector` already gives for six sprinting players, rather than widening the pool —
and it is a design call this lane does not own.

**Headroom for DOOR-1's bang and for voice**: the reservation arithmetic is unchanged (5 voice
speakers are still reserved for a full six-player lobby, against the two this game actually has),
so the bang has the same standing it had. What it does **not** have is protection from the heap:
under a forty-prop collapse the pool is saturated and the bang can be stolen like anything else.
That is the same finding as the 39 %, and the same fix.

## 8. Open for the orchestrator

- **A non-host peer hears no prop impact, and that is the one thing in this packet that is not
  finished.** `ObservedSpeedMps` gives a streamed prop a speed, but Godot reports a frozen
  kinematic body no contact at all, so there is nothing to give the speed to. Closing it means
  putting the impact on the wire — the cheapest shape is one flag on the existing Loose
  transition, or a tiny impact RPC on `PropChannel`. **In a two-player hide-and-seek game where
  the seeker's only remote sense is what they can hear, this is a gameplay bug, not a polish
  item.** The suite prints it every run so it cannot be forgotten.
- **A place and a throw are the same transition on the wire** (CARRY-1's own finding), so a
  remote peer plays the shared `Whoosh` for both and never hears the set-down tick. One bit on
  `ApplyPropState` fixes it and would fix the bullet above at the same time — worth doing as one
  packet rather than two.
- **`ProtocolVersion` is now 16.** Any other wave-2 lane that bumps it will conflict with this,
  and the merge must keep ONE bump with both reasons, not two.
- **`SfxLab.PoolSize` 14 → 18 and `LoopPoolSize` 5 → 1.** Anyone who wants a sustained positional
  layer (a fridge hum, a PA bed) now has exactly one loop slot and must argue with
  `AudioVoiceBudget` rather than just taking one.
- **Source-side impact limiting is unowned and now has a number behind it** (39 % of one-shots
  stolen under a forty-prop collapse). The precedent is `FootstepAudioDirector`.
- **`Run-AllTests.ps1` needs a desktop session for two suites now**, not one, and for a different
  reason than FP-1's — `ActorFx` early-outs on a headless display, so an audio suite must put a
  window on whichever peer it wants to listen with.
- **The holder's soft `Bump` on a scrape is not implemented** (§4), deliberately and with the
  reasoning stated.
- **Four `.wav.import` sidecars per capture were minted by the import pass** and are committed
  here rather than left as untracked churn, following INT-0's precedent.
