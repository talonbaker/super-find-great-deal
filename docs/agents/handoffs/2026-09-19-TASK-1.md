## 1. The mechanic, and the one decision inside it

Three bins. Eighteen objects, each of which is a colour AND a shape. **The bins ask for only one
of the two, and which one alternates by round.**

```
SortRule.For(round) = round % 2 == 1 ? Colour : Shape
```

`HideSeekTuning.MatchRounds` is 2 and the roles swap at the reset edge, so a match is
"each player does the job once, once each way" — and the second time is the hard one, because
the room is identical and the habit the first round built is now wrong. `AcrossOneMatch_BothRulesAreUsed`
asserts that against the tuning rather than against the literal 2.

**The bins never move; their MEANING changes.** That was the decision. Re-arranging the room at
the reset edge would make round 2 a fresh search; re-labelling the same three boxes makes it the
same job done against a learned habit, which is the concentration Talon asked for. It also means
the plates have to re-label, which they do on the poll after the reset edge with no catch-up path
— the rule is a pure function of a round index every peer already has.

### Scoring

An object counts when it has been **at rest inside a bin's volume for `SettleSec` (0.5 s)** and
the bin matches. Each object counts **once**: a counted id is remembered for the round, so taking
it out subtracts nothing and putting it back adds nothing. Wrong bin → one buzz, nothing
subtracted; the cost is the time.

Both halves of the once-only rule are deliberate and both are tested. Subtracting would make the
last ten seconds of a round about defending a score instead of doing the job; re-counting would
make one object worth eighteen points to anybody who noticed.

**Why a settle timer rather than counting on contact.** DOOR-1's burst shoves props, and a
carried object swings through a bin's mouth on the way past. Counting on entry would score both.
Half a second of STILLNESS is the cheapest honest statement of "the player meant that".

## 2. The architecture, and the one thing worth arguing with

**TASK-1 adds no wire field, no RPC and no channel.** The count rides `HideSeekWire`'s existing
slot — which is what the rename in commit 1 was about — and the RULE is derived. So the two
things that must happen on every peer (the buzz, and the plate flash) cannot be sent.

**Every peer therefore runs its own `SortTally`, and only the server's is the score.** The
objects and bins are authored identically everywhere and their transforms are replicated, so each
peer reaches the same verdicts from the same facts a beat apart. `SortRoom` on the server is the
one registered with `HideSeekDriver`; a client's copy can at worst make one extra noise and can
never change a score.

**It is measured rather than argued.** `Run-SortTest.ps1` asserts the per-process verdicts on all
three processes separately, and the planted "only the server derives" fault (§4) produces exactly
the failure that design risks — server 2/1, clients 0/0 — **with the wire count still reading a
perfectly correct 2 on both peers.** That is the shape of the bug, and it is what the suite is
pointed at.

**Rest is read from the replicated transform, not from the registry**: `HolderPeerId == 0` plus
"it has not moved since the last poll" (`RestEpsilonM` 0.005 m at 60 Hz = 0.3 m/s). One rule on
every peer rather than an authoritative branch and a cosmetic one that could disagree.

**Counting runs only in `Seeking`.** Program §5: progress freezes at the Found tick. The loop
freezes its own copy (`SortsAtFound`) and `SortRoom` stops feeding it, so the strip and the wall
stop moving at the instant the door goes — including for a hider who keeps sorting out of
momentum. Nothing special happens at the burst: an object it throws into a bin cannot score
because it is not at rest.

## 3. What was found by running it, rather than by review

### 3.1 The fourth payment for one engine trap, and the first time it was avoided

`TaskRoom.tscn` is instanced into `Supermarket.tscn`, and **a nested PackedScene instance's
exported C# script properties are silently dropped on this Godot/Mono build.** CARRY-1 §6
measured that exact shape (it is why `BurstDoor` finds the room light by name rather than through
an `[Export] NodePath`), after `PropManager.AuthoredKindOf` (2026-07), `Carryable.LoadLiftM` and
`RoundClock.Room` (CLOCK-1 §3.1).

The packet asks for `SortItem` to carry `Colour` and `Shape` enums "authored in the scene". An
`[Export]` would have produced **eighteen red cubes, in exactly the right places, with no error
anywhere** — and a level file that reads correctly in a diff. So neither fact is an export:

| Fact | Where it comes from | Why that survives |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | the suffix of the `NetworkedProp`'s node name (`Sort_000_Red`) | a node name is native. It is also the rule `.claude/rules/godot-scenes.md` states after CLOCK-1, and it makes `TaskRoom.tscn` self-describing: the line says `Sort_007_Blue` and its child instances `SortBall.tscn` |
| Shape | the `Body`'s collider, via `Carryable.ShapeFromCollider` | SFX-1's one table, already the source of the wire kind and the sound profile. A fourth reader of one native fact cannot disagree with the other three |
| Bin slot | the digits after `SortBin_` | same rule, same reason |

**The material tag falls out of the geometry rather than being a second thing to keep in step.**
A cubic `BoxShape3D` → `Crate` → Wood; an 0.08 m `SphereShape3D` → `Produce`; a
`CylinderShape3D` → `Can` → Tin. The packet's "cube = Wood, ball = Produce, can = Tin" is
therefore not configured anywhere; it is the collider.

**It is instrumented rather than trusted.** `SortRoom` prints a census on its first poll, and the
suite asserts it:

```
[sort] task room ready: 18 sortable(s), 3 bin(s), colours red=6 blue=6 yellow=6,
       shapes cube=6 ball=6 can=6, unresolved=0, rule=COLOUR, peer=1
[sort] items: 1004=Red/Cube 1005=Blue/Ball 1006=Yellow/Can 1007=Blue/Cube ...
```

That line is the instrument the export trap would have failed on the first run rather than the
first playtest.

### 3.2 A render corrected a constant a green suite could not, again

`EveryPlateWord_FitsThePlate` passed: at `font_size` 28 the arithmetic said YELLOW spans 0.54 m
of a 0.66 m plate. **The first capture showed it touching both edges**, exactly as CLOCK-1 §3.3's
`3 SORTED` did one wall over. `font_size` is 24 now (0.46 m of arithmetic, ~14 px of margin each
side measured off the render). A character count is an AVERAGE and is wrong in both directions;
the bound in xUnit keeps the COPY from growing and only a photograph can tell you the size is
right.

### 3.3 A scripted fixture can stall on the level and log absolutely nothing

The first full smoke run delivered two objects and then stood still for fifty seconds. **Nothing
was logged, because a press that is never made is never refused** — `grab denied` is the first
thing anyone greps for and it cannot fire for a request that was not sent.

The sortables sit on a 1.4 m-wide crate, so a body walking at an object on the far column is
stopped by the crate itself with the object still **1.40 m** away, outside the 1.2 m arrive radius
copied from `ScriptedCarryIntentSource` (whose props sit on the floor). The discriminator was the
bot's own JSONL: position byte-identical at t = 25, 31 and 38 s with an empty hand.

`ScriptedSortIntentSource.FetchStandM` is 1.8 m now, derived from the SERVER's reach
(`SandboxAvatar.PickupRadius` 1.5 + `PropManager.GrabRangeTolerance` 0.75 = 2.25 m in 3D) rather
than copied. **Generalises: an arrive radius is a fact about the furniture the target sits on.**

Two more from the same run, both in the rules file:

- **The place is gated on the distance to the DROP POINT, not to a stand-here marker.** The
  server measures a place from the HAND against 1.65 m, and a body merely near a marker can be
  over two metres from the spot it is aiming at. Both of that run's deliveries were refused
  `TooFarToPlace` on their first attempt and landed on the retry — a fixture that worked by
  accident.
- **A delivered object must be LEFT ALONE.** Without a guard, the tick after a hand-off lands the
  brain sees an empty hand, decides it still owes the step its object, walks back and picks it
  straight out of the bin — which makes "am I still holding it" true again and wedges the script
  on its second item.

### 3.4 `@($list)` on a `List[object]` throws in Windows PowerShell 5.1

```
Argument types do not match
+     return @($out)
```

The throw is at the wrapping `@(...)`, not at anything inside, and the suite reported it from
three statements later — it read exactly like a type error in the assertion that followed.
`List[string]` is fine (`Run-PlaceTest.ps1` has used one for months); it is the `[object]`
instantiation that bites. Cost about twenty minutes. In the rules file.

### 3.5 A windowed client loses a 1.2 s join race to a headless one

`Capture-SortRoom.ps1`'s third run came back reading **`FOUND · YOU SEEK · ROUND 1`** from an
empty search room, with `hider=False` in the door's own line, while the two runs before it were
fine. Roles are assigned by join order, and creating a window and a renderer costs several
seconds that a `--headless` boot does not pay — so a `Start-Sleep 1200` head start is not one.
The harness gates the second client on the first one's own `[client] connected as peer` line now.
Same rule as everywhere else in `tests/`: gate on the event, never on a guess about how long it
takes.

### 3.6 `found` is a LEVEL and it latches, which round 2 notices

The capture harness's shape pass photographed `FOUND · YOU HIDE · ROUND 2` fourteen seconds into
a round that was supposed to be running. `--round-script`'s `found` verb is the BIN — a level,
not a press (ROUND-1: "a bin does not pulse") — so it stays on and round 2 leaves `Seeking` on
its first tick. A `lost@N` before the second `start` is what clears it. Worth knowing for any
lane that scripts more than one round.

## 5. Deviations from the packet, each with one line of why

1. **Neither of `SortItem`'s two facts is an `[Export]`.** The packet says "authored in the
   scene"; an export on a node inside an instanced sub-scene reads back as its C# default on this
   build (§3.1), so the colour is the node name and the shape is the collider — both native, both
   authored, and the census line proves it every run.
2. **Node names are `Sort_000_Red`, not `Sort_000`.** Same reason: the name is where the colour
   lives. The three-digit index is the packet's and still fixes the adoption order.
3. **Three prefabs and a runtime tint, not nine coloured prefabs.** A sortable's colour IS the
   rule, so the value the player sees and the value `SortRule.Matches` tests are one value; there
   is no authored albedo that could drift from the enum.
4. **The three sortable prefabs are NOT in `SupermarketWorldSelfTest.SectionScenes`.** The packet
   asks for them; each is a `Carryable` and builds its own outline shell in `_Ready` exactly as
   `Crate.tscn` does, so listing one asserts a failure by construction. They are covered one level
   up: `SortItem` is authored directly in `TaskRoom.tscn`, so the room's own count walks it to the
   leaf. `SortBin.tscn` and `SupplyCrate.tscn` ARE in the list and the plant proves it.
5. **`Sfx.SortGood = 46` / `SortBad = 47`, and no existing recipe was reused.** The brief offered
   `Note` and `RoundBuzz`; both fire ONCE a round (Confirm, and the hide buzzer) and these fire up
   to eighteen times in one Seeking phase. Reusing one would teach the player the sound means
   nothing and make the real Confirm note unreadable when it finally came. Both new ones are
   deliberately shorter than the cues they sit beside, and a test pins that.
6. **Every peer runs a tally; only the server's scores.** The packet says "Scoring (server,
   engine-free rule + one adapter)". The rule and the score are server-authoritative exactly as
   asked; the CLIENTS run a copy because the buzz and the flash have to reach them and TASK-1 was
   told to take no channel. §2 has the argument and §4 has the planted fault that tests it.
7. **`--sort-script` grabs BY ID rather than through `InteractTargeting.Pick`.** Eighteen objects
   sit 0.22 m apart in one crate and an aim cone asked to choose between them picks a different
   one on a loaded machine. Same split `BotHarness.MaybePlace` already documents for
   `--carry-place`.
8. **The `--round-script` verb `towers:N` is renamed `sorts:N`**, with the three scripts that use
   it updated. One name, not two.
9. **`BotHarness.Sample` gains `roundSorts`.** Not in the packet. Without it the smoke cannot see
   the one thing the sort puts on a wire, and "the count agrees on every peer" is the assertion
   that distinguishes a working sort from three peers each quietly scoring their own.
10. **A fourth capture** (`bins-shape-round2.png`) beyond the two asked for — the re-labelling is
    the mechanic and one photograph of one rule does not show it.
11. **`tests/Capture-SortRoom.ps1` is new and unregistered**, the same shape as CLOCK-1's
    `Capture-RoundClock.ps1`, so the captures are reproducible rather than being files somebody
    once made. It shares port 7907 with the suite and the two never run together (both take the
    machine mutex).

## 6. The bible check

```
Bibles applied:  MECHANICS (the sort resolves its own state, owns a timer and is the hider's
                 entire score), INTERACTION (the player acts on the bins, and the bins answer),
                 LEVEL (eighteen objects, three bins and a crate arranged into a space a player
                 walks and works in).

Items checked:   MECHANICS section 1 (boundary conditions) - the settle latch fires at
                 >= SettleSec and is tested at 0.4 s and at 0.6 s; a dwell restarts on a bin
                 change rather than accumulating across bins; SortsCompleted is clamped at zero
                 in the loop and again in the copy; a negative, NaN or absurd dt advances
                 nothing; an out-of-range bin slot answers false rather than throwing.
                 MECHANICS section 3 (simultaneous events) - all eighteen objects settling on
                 one tick is a test, and the order is the sighting list's, which is the authored
                 node order and therefore identical on every peer.
                 MECHANICS section 4 (idempotency) - the whole once-only rule. An object counts
                 once however many times it is re-placed, the wrong-bin buzz fires once per stay
                 and re-arms only on a pick-up, and a counted object is silent afterwards in
                 either bin. Two tests and one planted fault.
                 MECHANICS section 6 (numeric rules, the extremes) - zero objects, one, eighteen;
                 both rules over the same layout; a round index of 0, -1 and int.MinValue.
                 INTERACTION section 2 (feedback at the moment of the trigger) - a correct sort
                 flashes the plate and ticks, a wrong one buzzes, both on every peer, both within
                 the settle latch rather than a frame later.
                 INTERACTION section 5 (multiplayer contention) - decided and stated: the SERVER's
                 tally is the score and a client's copy is presentation only, so two peers cannot
                 disagree about a number anybody is judged on.
                 INTERACTION section 6 (reversibility) - stated explicitly: a sort is one-time per
                 object per round and resets at the round boundary, with the objects.
                 INTERACTION section 8.2 (the redundant-channel rule) - the bin says what it wants
                 on THREE channels: the word, the plate's tint on a colour round, and an unshaded
                 icon on a shape round. This is the one place in the game where a colour IS a
                 rule, so a player who cannot separate this red from this yellow must not be
                 locked out of the mechanic; the palette is also separated by luminance (~0.30 /
                 0.24 / 0.72) and not by hue alone.
                 LEVEL section 3 (spawn/placement) and section 4 (path) - the crate and the bins
                 are about 5 m apart, so a trip is a real walk and being found mid-carry is the
```

## 8. Open for the orchestrator

- **The sort is reachable only through `--sort-script` and a human's hands.** There is no
  in-world instruction anywhere: a player walking into the task room for the first time sees
  three labelled boxes and a crate and has to infer the job. That is probably right for a
  playtest and it is worth knowing it was not designed.
- **The wrong-bin buzz is derived per peer and could in principle disagree by a frame.** It has
  not in four runs (three processes, identical verdicts every time), and the score cannot
  disagree because it is the server's. If it ever does, the fix is one bit on the wire — the same
  bit SFX-1 §3.2 needs for the remote impact, which is still open.
- **`SortsCompleted` is a `ushort` on the wire and eighteen objects is the whole crate**, so the
  clamp cannot bite today. SHELF-1 or a bigger crate would want a look.
- **Talon may change the whole thing after the first ride** (the packet says so). The parts most
  likely to move are all one-liners: `SortRule.For`'s alternation, `SortTally.SettleSec`, the
  three `SortPalette` colours and the bins' transforms in `TaskRoom.tscn`. Nothing about the
  scoring or the wire has to move with them.
- **The two audio cues have never been heard by a human.** Every gate here is headless; the
  recipes are asserted for headroom, length and spectral separation in xUnit, which is a claim
  about the buffers and not about the mix.
- **`Capture-SortRoom.ps1` shares 7907 with `Run-SortTest.ps1`.** Harmless (it is unregistered
  and manual, and both take the machine mutex), stated because CLOCK-1 stated the same thing.
