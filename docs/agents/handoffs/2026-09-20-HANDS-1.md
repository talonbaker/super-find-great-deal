## 2. The design, and the one thing it is for

Lethal Company draws arms in a fixed pose per item: the hands say *what* you carry and never
*where* you hold it. R.E.P.O. draws none at all and implies them with a tether. Both are right for
their games and wrong for this one, because after FEEL-1 the object is held at the point you
grabbed it and the whole verb is *putting a specific thing in a specific spot by hand*. **So the
hand's only job is to show the grab point**, and every decision below falls out of that.

- **Idle**: both hands low in the frame, in from the edges, open, **still**. No bob, no sway, no
  breathing idle — SICK-1's law is that the first-person view contains no motion the player did
  not ask for, and a perfectly steady pair of hands is also a fixed reference the eye can hold,
  which is a motion-sickness reducer in its own right. The pose is derived against the lens rather
  than chosen by eye: at 0.45 m a 75° vertical lens is 0.345 m tall and 0.613 m wide on 16:9, so
  y = −0.26 is 75 % down the frame and x = ±0.34 is 55 % out.
- **Reach**: the near hand lerps to the target in 120 ms, straight, no clip.
- **Hold**: it closes (one mesh swap) at the grab point and **rides the prop** — through the
  wheel's distance, through RMB rotation, through the holder walking.
- **Two hands** above the threshold, on opposite faces, straddling the grab point.
- **Poke**: a press sends the open hand out, dwells 60 ms, comes back.
- **Release**: the hand opens and returns in 150 ms — slower than the reach, because going for a
  thing is a decision and losing it is a consequence.
- **No IK, no finger rig, no animation clips.** Position and roll come from the grab point and the
  surface it sits on. Simple over juice.

## 3. The deviation that matters: the grab point is INSIDE the prop

**This is the one place the packet's letter and its intent came apart, and the arithmetic is why.**

The packet says: *"it closes at the grab point and stays attached to that point … Hand distance
from the grab point ≤ 1 cm every frame (assert it)."* Taken literally that is a hand drawn at
`Body.GlobalTransform * grabLocal`, and **for any well-aimed grab that point is inside the
object** — at the centre of a crate, in the ordinary case. Read `NetworkedProp.BindToHolderRayHold`:

```
_holdDistanceM = ClampHoldDistance((prop.Origin - eye).Dot(dir), min, max);
onProp = CarryHold.GrabPointOnProp(prop.Origin, HoldPoint(eye, dir, _holdDistanceM), r);
```

The hold distance is the projection of the prop's **centre** onto the view ray, so the ray point
is the foot of the perpendicular from the centre — the closest point on the ray to the middle of
the object. `GrabPointOnProp` pulls it onto the bounding sphere only when the ray **misses** that
sphere. A 0.44 m crate has a bounding radius of 0.381 m, so aiming anywhere near it leaves the
grab point interior, and a hand there is a hand nobody can see. Talon judges this concept **from
the captures**; a capture of a hand inside a crate shows him nothing.

**So the hand goes to the grab point's own line of sight where it leaves the prop**
(`HandReach.SurfacePoint`): keep the grab point's component ACROSS the view, replace its depth
with the surface. That keeps every property the assertion is about — it is the grab point across
the view, it rides the prop's rotation, it moves with the wheel — and adds the one the player
needs.

**The assertion moved with it, and it is stronger, not weaker.** Two bars, both computed by the
probe from the PROP's live transform, FEEL-1's own recorded grab point and the eye, never from the
node's internal offset:

| Bar | What it catches |
|---|---|
| **the hand (or the pair's midpoint) within 1 cm of the grab point ACROSS the view** | a wrong attach offset, a stale local space, an update ordered before the camera |
| **no hand more than 5 cm outside the prop's bounding sphere** | a hand floating off the object, which bar 1 alone permits |

A raw 3-D distance would have reported the prop's radius every frame and measured nothing at all.
**Sideways is where an attach bug shows up, and sideways is what `--hands-plant-offset` moves** —
see §6.

**"Across the view" is two axes and they are judged differently, because the design treats them
differently:**

- **UP** — the pair's height is the GRAB POINT's height, one hand or two. Grab a crate low and you
  carry it low.
- **SIDE** — one hand sits ON the grab point sideways. **Two hands sit on the object's two side
  faces**, so their midpoint is the OBJECT's centre line, not the grab point's: grip a box
  slightly off-centre and your hands are still on its faces. Measuring a two-handed midpoint
  against the grab point sideways would be asserting a pose nobody wants — and it is not a
  loophole, because the plant pushes every attached hand along exactly this axis.

**One more thing the two-handed case forced, and it is a real correction rather than a
loosening.** A flat radial clamp of every hand to `ArmLengthM` drags both hands of a two-handed
grip inward along the view ray every frame: a 0.44 m crate held at the 1.2 m ceiling has its side
faces at `sqrt(1.2² + 0.235²)` = **1.26 m** from the eye, and your shoulders are not at your eye.
`HandReach.HoldReachM(propRadius)` = arm + that prop's bounding radius is the bound for a hand on
something already held — and it is still tight, because FEEL-1 clamps the grab point itself to the
arm, so it is a guard against a bug rather than a shape anyone can reach. A POKE still clamps at
the bare arm, because that one is a reach at the world.

## 4. The two-hand rule, and the arm that is not a second number

**`HandReach.ArmLengthM` returns `CarryHold.HoldMaxBaseM`.** Packet item 4, and
`TheArmLengthIsFeel1sHoldCeiling_NotASecondNumber` is the test that keeps it that way. The hand is
clamped to it every frame, which is the honest reason FEEL-1's clamp exists: past an arm's length
a carry stops being a carry and becomes telekinesis.

**FEEL-1 defined no two-hand threshold**, so this lane states one, with the shipped props either
side of it and the arithmetic written at the constant:

| prop | longest axis | bounding radius | mass | hands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Can (`Can.tscn`, r 0.035 × h 0.12) | **0.12 m** | 0.078 m | 0.35 kg | **1** |
| Produce (sphere r 0.08) | **0.16 m** | 0.139 m | 0.25 kg | **1** |
| Cereal box (0.19 × 0.28 × 0.06) | **0.28 m** | 0.172 m | 0.4 kg | **2** |
| Crate (0.44 cube) | **0.44 m** | 0.381 m | 1.0 kg | **2** |

`OneHandSpanM` is **0.20 m**, in the gap the props leave (produce 0.16, cereal 0.28), and it is a
SPAN rather than a volume because the failure it names is leverage: a thing longer than your hand
is long cannot be controlled from one grip, it levers out.

**`OneHandMassKg` is 2.0 kg and nothing shipped today trips it.** Stated rather than hidden: every
one-handed prop is under 0.4 kg and every two-handed one is already over the span, so the mass arm
never decides on this build and the captures are judging the span arm. It exists because size and
weight come apart the moment Talon's real meshes arrive — a tin of paint is can-sized and nobody
one-hands it — and because a rule with one clause has to be rewritten rather than extended when
they do. `AHeavySmallThingStillTakesTwoHands` is the only place it is exercised.

**The straddle axis is across the VIEW, not one of the prop's own axes.** You put your hands on
whichever two faces are to your left and right; walk round the crate and they are different faces.
An axis fixed to the prop eventually puts a hand behind it. The half-span is the **support
function of the oriented box** (`HandReach.SupportHalfExtent`) rather than the bounding radius —
for a 0.44 m cube those differ by 0.16 m, which is a hand floating in mid-air — and it follows a
turned prop, because a crate spun 45° really is wider across the view.

## 5. Three seams that had to exist, each with the measurement behind it

### 5.1 `LensAimIntentSource` — a capture bot's lens has to aim the game

FP-1's `--fp-look` aims the **lens**, and the client's own pick resolves against it. But
`MoveIntent.AimYaw`/`AimPitch` still come from the brain, and **those** are what
`NetworkedProp.HoldPointNow` holds the object along. So a bot with a lens pointed one way and a
brain walking another carries its crate off screen, and every capture in this suite would have
been a photograph of an empty room. `LensAimIntentSource` forwards the brain's intent with the
lens's angles substituted, declares `SuppliesLook` so `SandboxAvatar.IntentSource`'s setter does
not wrap it in `TravelFacingIntentSource` (which would refill the aim from the walk direction and
silently delete the point), and forwards `IsHumanInput` rather than defaulting it.

**It is applied ONLY under `--hands-selftest`.** `--fp-look`'s behaviour for
`Run-FirstPersonTest` is byte-for-byte untouched — but the inconsistency is general and is written
down here rather than left for the next capture lane to rediscover.

`BotAchievementGateTests.OnlyLocalInputClaimsToBeHumanInput` went **red on this addition** and that
is the test doing its job: it enumerates every `IIntentSource` that declares `IsHumanInput` and
demands a reason for each. The decorator is now in its expected set with the reason, and asserted
on an instance (forwards false for a bot, true for a human) rather than by declaration.

### 5.2 `HandsProbeIntentSource` — a 1.5 m latch cannot reach a 1.6 m button

`ScriptedGotoIntentSource.ArriveRadius` is **1.5 m**, which is right for a waypoint and fatal
here. This fixture needs a can, a crate **and** a pressable inside reach from ONE standing spot,
and the smallest of those reaches is `RoundButton.PressRadius` — 1.6 m measured in 3-D to a button
**1.15 m up the wall**, i.e. a horizontal budget of `sqrt(1.6² − 1.15²)` = **1.11 m**. A latch that
stops 1.5 m short can never get there, and a fixture whose staging depends on where a 1.5 m sphere
happens to cross an approach line goes red the day somebody re-tunes a walk. SICK-1 added
`--goto-patrol` one packet earlier for the same reason and wrote down that every other brain in
the repo was disqualified for its measurement too.

This brain walks to one point and stands within **15 cm** of it. It takes **no irreversible
action** — standing is re-decided every tick — so W6-3's rule (compute from a predicted position,
never act on one) does not bind, which is the difference from `ScriptedGotoIntentSource`'s one-way
latch and why that one has to confirm on the authority.

### 5.3 The poke is hooked at the ONE place a press happens

`RoundControls.ClientRequestPress` is what `RoundButton.Press` calls **and** what `--press` calls
(BTN-1's own handoff says so). Hooking there makes it impossible to press a button in this game
without the hand reaching for it, costs every non-first-person peer nothing (`Local` is null), and
resolves WHICH button through the avatar's own aim-aware `FindNearestPressable` — the same pick
the press resolved against, so the hand cannot reach for a different button than the one pressed.
A press fired from across the room moves nothing, which is correct: there is nothing there to
touch.

```
        held prop 2: bounding radius 0.381 m, 1.00 kg -> 2 hand(s)

```

**Read `worstLateral` first.** It is the hand's (or the pair's midpoint's) distance from the grab
point ACROSS the line of sight, every frame the hold is settled, and it is **zero to four decimal
places** — not "within the bar", but exactly on the point, because the hand's pose is derived from
the same live transform the measurement is. What that number defends is the ORDERING and the
SPACE: the hands run at `ProcessPriority` 1000 (after the camera) and store a pose in the lens's
frame, and either mistake puts centimetres into this column.

**`worstOffSurface` is 0.0150 m, which is `HandReach.PalmProudM` exactly** — the hand rests one
palm half-thickness proud of the prop's bounding sphere and never further, on either the one-hand
or the two-hand path. That is the bar doing its job and also a small proof that the straddle's
half-span arithmetic agrees with the bounding radius it is normalised to.

### 6b. What the real runs of the suite found — four things, and two of them were the instrument

**The suite was red on its first honest run and every one of the three causes was worth the run.**
None of them was the feature; two were the fixture and one was the measurement.

#### The instrument was reading the reach and calling it a defect

The first seven held frames reported **26.0, 22.4, 18.8, 15.2, 11.6, 8.0, 4.3 cm** of
hand-to-grab-point error, then settled. That is a clean linear decay over about 120 ms — it is
`ReachSec`, doing exactly what it was asked to do, measured by a bar that had no idea the hand was
deliberately somewhere else.

`FirstPersonHands.Settled` is the fix and the probe skips those frames, **counting them** so a
build that never settled fails the sample floor rather than reading as green. The general shape is
the one this repo has paid for before: *a per-frame invariant must say which frames it is about,
and a transition is not a defect.*

#### "The biggest free carryable in reach" picked up the room

`[hands-selftest] STAGING ... largest=prop 1018 r=0.637 at 1.37 m` — and prop 1018 is
`SearchRoom/Stock/Bin_0`, a **6 kg floor bin**, which is a perfectly legal carryable and was
0.4 m nearer the standing spot than the crate the fixture had seeded. The suite then asserted the
two-hand rule about a bin.

The fix is `--hands-props "x,y,z;x,y,z"`: the fixture NAMES the two spots it staged and the probe
takes the nearest free carryable to each, within half a metre. **By position, not by prop id** —
HOLD-1 recorded three renumberings in two days, because an authored prop's id is a function of
every other prop's node name — and **not by a size heuristic**, because a heuristic about "the
biggest thing here" is a claim about the room rather than about the fixture. The suite now has one
definition of each spot, which both the seed and the probe read.

**And the sharp half: the run in between went GREEN while still holding the bin.** An edit that
renamed the two picks but left the heuristic behind them intact produced a run reporting
`worstLateralM=0.0000`, `holds=2`, and a clean one-hand/two-hand split — because a 6 kg, 0.82 m
floor bin **is** genuinely two-handed, so every bar in the suite was satisfied by the wrong
object. The capture written on that run is labelled `two-hand-crate` and is a photograph of a bin.
*A per-frame geometric bar cannot tell you that the fixture staged the wrong thing*; only naming
what the fixture is about can, which is why the STAGING line prints the prop it resolved and its
distance from the named spot before anything else is asserted.

#### An absolute mark caught the bot mid-walk, under load

`the probe was still moving at 1.56 m/s at t=4.51s`, with nothing in reach — on a run where the
subject itself was perfect (`worstLateralM=0`, two holds, 504 frames). The walk is 3.4 m at the
2.4 m/s browse pace, about 1.4 s, but the probe's clock starts at `_Ready` and the connect, the
spawn and the first physics ticks all land in front of it; under another lane's suite they land
later. The mark was a guess about when the bot would arrive, and the suite then reported the delay
as a failure of the fixture's props.

**Every beat is now an OFFSET from the frame the probe actually settled** — still, and able to
reach both staged objects — with a 12 s absolute deadline for the settle itself and a duration
derived from that schedule rather than typed beside it (FIX-1's rule, in this repo's own rules
file). On the run that followed, the probe settled at **t = 1.63 s** and the whole script ran from
there. *Gate on the event, never on a guess about when it happens.*

#### The prop-bulk walk over-measured a can by five times

`[hands] grabbed prop 1: longest axis 0.598 m, 0.35 kg -> TWO hand(s)` — about a **0.12 m can**.
Walking a `Carryable`'s subtree for `VisualInstance3D`s and merging their AABBs picks up more than
the shape mesh, and each hand then sat **25 cm off the can's surface** with the two-hand rule
firing on a one-handed object.

**`Carryable.BoundingRadiusM` is the shipped authority on how big a prop is** — it is computed in
that class from the mesh it actually chose, and FEEL-1 derives `HoldMin` and the holder clearance
from it. So the walk now supplies only the SHAPE (which way the bulk is distributed, which a
bounding radius cannot say) and is **scaled so its magnitude IS that radius**. The walk also takes
`MeshInstance3D` only and skips the `Outline` shell. The answer now agrees with every other
consumer of that number by construction rather than by hoping the walk found the right nodes.

0.030 m slab has nothing else to show. That is a fact about the PLACEHOLDER, not about the
placement — the reach maths puts the hands exactly where a real hand mesh's palm would go, and a
mesh with fingers that wrap forward is what makes the grip read. It is the first thing to look at
when Talon's meshes arrive.

---

## 7. What was deliberately NOT done

- **No authored meshes, nothing under `assets/models/`.** Talon's ruling, and `HandVisual` is the
  one place a real hand replaces the primitive: two sizes, two methods, and an axis contract
  (+Y back of hand, −Z fingers, origin in the palm) so the swap touches no reach logic.
- **No other-player hands** (ruling R5). The avatar's existing arm substrate keeps doing what it
  does for spectators and the CCTV feed. A later packet can drive that arm to the same grab point
  once SYNC settles ownership.
- **No IK, no finger rig, no animation clips, no easing.** Straight lerps and one mesh swap.
- **`Carryable` was not touched**, and neither was anything else under `scripts/game/props/`
  beyond two read-only properties for the grab point. PHYS-1 is live in those files. The prop's
  half-extents — which the straddle needs and `Carryable` does not expose — are read off its own
  meshes in `FirstPersonHands`, once per grab, rather than by adding a property there.
- **No crosshair.** SICK-1 recommended one and flagged it as a design call Talon has not made. The
  hands are a fixed reference at the bottom of the frame, which is the other half of that
  recommendation and is what this packet was for.

## 8. The bible check

```
Bibles applied:  INTERACTION (the player acts on it: the hand is the feedback for a grab, a
                 release, a refusal and a press), MECHANICS (the hand resolves its own state
                 every frame from the hold and gives it up when the hold does)
Items checked:   INTERACTION 1 (an interactable reads as interactable before you touch it --
                 unchanged; the shimmer and the chip are FEEL-1's and this adds a hand that
                 arrives after the click rather than a second affordance before it),
                 2 (a refusal is perceivable -- a hold the world breaks now OPENS the hand and
                 returns it over 150 ms, slower than the reach, so losing an object looks
                 different from putting one down),
                 3 (a physical verb should visibly do the physical thing -- this is the whole
                 packet: the object is held at the point you grabbed it and now you can SEE
                 that point, and the wheel reads as your arm extending because the hand rides
                 the grab point out with it),
                 5 and 7 (one control, one meaning -- no control was added at all),
                 9.1 (effect is a function of (verb, target) resolved at use time -- the poke
                 resolves its target through the same aim-aware pick the press resolved
                 against, so the hand can never reach for a different button than the one that
                 was pressed)
Result:          pass. Item 3 is the one that shaped the design: FEEL-1 made the hold physical
                 and the player had no way to see where they were holding it.
```

## 9. Deviations from the packet, each with its reason

1. **Base is `feat/2026-09-20-feel-1` @ `f086ed4`, not `integration/2026-09-19-mvp`.** The packet
   allowed this; INT-2 had still not merged FEEL-1/SICK-1/SOLO-1 when this lane's gates ran, so
   the merge the dispatch asked for **was not possible and was not done** — see §6 for exactly
   what that means for the SICK-1 interaction, which was read on SICK-1's branch and designed
   against rather than merged against.
2. **The 1 cm assertion is measured ACROSS the line of sight, not as a raw 3-D distance**, and the
   hand is drawn on the prop's surface rather than at the interior grab point. §3, with the
   arithmetic.
3. **`NetworkedProp` gained two read-only properties** (`GrabLocal`, `GrabPointWorld`). The packet
   allows "reading the grab point" and there was no accessor; `_grabLocal` was private.
4. **`RoundControls.ClientRequestPress` gained one line.** §5.3 — it is the only client-side point
   every press goes through, and no lane owns `scripts/game/round/` this wave.
5. **Two new intent sources** (§5.1, §5.2) rather than reusing `--fp-look` and `--goto-script`,
   each with the measurement that disqualified the shipped one.
6. **The break-hold capture is a RELEASE capture.** Staging a genuine world-blocked break needs a
   bot carrying a crate into a pillar for 0.8 s, which is `Run-CarryHoldTest`'s whole phase 2 and a
   second fixture here. The hand's open-and-return is ONE code path for every way a hold ends —
   placed, thrown, taken, broken — so the picture is the same picture; it is labelled honestly and
   the path is pinned by the timing tests rather than by the photograph.
7. **The two-hand threshold is defined here**, because FEEL-1 defined none (packet item 5 assumed
   it had). §4.
