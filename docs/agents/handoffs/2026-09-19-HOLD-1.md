## 2. The board

`scenes/game/world/supermarket/HoldingBoard.tscn`, instanced in `HoldingRoom.tscn` at
**(−3.1, 1.55, −4.94)**, identity basis. 3.6 × 2.2 m panel, no collision, no colour in the scene
file. Painted by `scripts/game/round/HoldingBoard.cs`; **what it says** is
`scripts/game/round/HoldingBoardModel.cs`, which has no engine in it at all.

**It is CLOCK-1's `RoundClock` with more lines on it**, by instruction and in construction: same
panel recipe, same `UiTokens`-at-runtime colouring (so a lost binding renders white and screams),
same hidden-until-synced rule, and **the same single 10 Hz poll** — `RoundAudio` gained a
`Boards` list beside its `Clocks` list. Two lists rather than one because a clock is also an
audio emitter (`Fire` plays the positional layer once per clock) and a board is furniture that
reads; folding them together would quietly give every cue an extra voice out of a wall that is
not a clock.

### 2.1 What is on it

```
MATCH 1 · ROUND 2 OF 2 · HOLDING
Board · NEXT: YOU SEEK · 3
Corner · NEXT: HIDES · 168


ROUND 1 OF 2 · Board hid · 3 sorted · found at 0:12 · Corner +168
```

- **Header** — `MATCH n · ROUND r OF 2 · PHASE`, off the **live** `view.Round` through the tuning.
- **Rows** — one per human on the wire: name, role cell, cumulative score. Four authored labels,
  blanked when unused; a fifth human is reported on an `Overflow` line rather than dropped.
- **Footer** — the card. `HideSeekText.BoardFooter`, new.

### 2.2 The role cell during Holding is the NEXT round's role, and there is no second column

`HideSeekLoop.EnterHolding` swaps `HiderPeerId` and `SeekerPeerId` **at the Tally → Holding
commit**, so while the two of them are standing at the rack the view already names next round's
hider. A "role this round" column and a "next round" column would print the same value from the
same two fields and invite a reader to believe they were two facts.

**The pronoun is the ONLY thing that differs between two peers' copies of this wall.** Your own
row says `NEXT: YOU HIDE`; everyone else's says `NEXT: HIDES`. Both players are therefore told
about the swap, which is what the packet asks for, and nobody has to work out which row is theirs.
`Rows_DifferBetweenPeersOnlyInThePronoun` pins it and the smoke normalises exactly that one
substitution before comparing.

### 2.3 The board reads the CARD, never `view.Scores`, for the result — and `view.Scores` for the column

The orchestrator's ruling, and it is not a style choice: the Start that begins the next match
zeroes the live map while the card still holds the result, so a footer built from the live totals
would silently become "ADA WON 0–0" the instant the next round began. The **cumulative-score
column** is the opposite case and correctly goes to zero.
`Footer_KeepsTheMatchTotalsAfterTheNextStartZeroesTheLiveScores` asserts both off ONE view,
because the whole point is that they disagree.

### 2.4 `HideSeekText.BoardFooter` — MatchLine plus exactly one case

`MatchLine` is empty during Holding after an **ordinary** round, which is right for a one-line
strip (the live phase line is the better thing to show there) and wrong for a board, whose header
already carries the phase. `BoardFooter` calls the same private `ChooseMatchSentence` and fills in
that one hole with `RoundTallyLine`. **The phase rules stay in one place**, which is what MATCH-1
insisted on; the capture `board-2-...png` is that case working.

### 2.5 Row order is ASCENDING PEER ID, not join order — a deviation, with a reason

The packet asks for join order. **Join order is not on the wire.** The only roster a board may
read is `view.Scores` (anything else breaks the late joiner's one-message guarantee) and an
`ImmutableDictionary` is a hash trie with no insertion order to recover. Ascending peer id gives
the property a reader actually needs: **stable for the whole session, identical on every peer, and
it does not move when the roles swap.** Ordering by role would have moved every row every round.

### 2.6 The panel's width, and where the numbers come from

`HoldingBoardLayout` restates CLOCK-1's **measured** 0.64 advance-per-glyph-height (the panel
spanned 275 px and an eight-character line spanned 283 in its capture) as a named constant instead
of multiplying it into two magic numbers.

| Line | `font_size` | glyph | chars it holds | at 4 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rows | 40 | 0.20 m | 25 | ~44 px of a 1152-line frame |
| header / footer | 32 | 0.16 m | 32 | ~35 px |

Rows were **44 at first**. At 44 the panel held 23 characters and `ADA · NEXT: YOU HIDE · 7` is
24 — the shortest plausible row in the game was already being shrunk, and every real name would
have been shrunk further. 40 makes the fit the exception rather than the default.
`MinScale = 0.30` is proved against the worst line the WIRE can produce, constructed rather than
reasoned about (`TheFooterFloorIsBelowTheWorstLineTheWireCanProduce`), with `TheFitCanReachItsFloor`
as the positive control.

## 6. The xUnit side, and a guard that was not being tested

`tests/unit/HoldingBoardTests.cs`, 32 tests. Two plants:

- **Footer totals forced to 0** (a card reading the live map after a reset): both footer tests
  red, plus eight of MATCH-1's. Correct.
- **`ids.Sort()` deleted: EVERY ordering assertion stayed GREEN.** That is CELEBRATE-1's trap
  arriving intact. `ImmutableDictionary<int,int>` enumerates in hash order, `int`'s hash IS the
  value, and small positive ints come out ascending anyway. Measured with four real peer ids from
  `tests/logs/`: they enumerate **233849852, 652333145, 1076010669, 1788870000 — already sorted.**
  So the sort is unobservable through the shipped wire and an ordering test written the obvious
  way proves nothing at all.

  **To prove a guard, first make its call site reach it.**
  `Rows_AreAscendingEvenWhenTheMapEnumeratesBackwards` builds the score map with a comparer that
  hashes to the negated key, **carries its own positive control** that the map really does
  enumerate differently, and goes red the moment the sort goes away. It also required fixing the
  test's own `View()` helper, which was calling `ToImmutableDictionary()` and silently rebuilding
  the map with the default comparer — with that rebuild in place the hostile test passed against a

## 7. A blank that never blanked, found by the CAPTURE and by nothing else

placeholder — a readout that looks like it has something to say and cannot say it.

Nothing in `tests/unit` could see it (the model returns the right strings; the bug is in the
pusher) and nothing in the smoke could either (`CurrentText` reports the same `_last*` fields the
early-out reads, so both halves agreed with each other while disagreeing with the wall). **The
first render was the only instrument that could.** Fixed with a sentinel no real line can equal.

## 11. What HOLD-1 did NOT do, and what is left

- **No new wire, no protocol bump, no new `Sfx` ordinal.** The board is derived on each peer from
  a message that already existed, which is why a late joiner is correct on its first frame and why
  this lane costs zero bytes.
- **Nothing HOLD-1 added to the −Z wall can steal a press from START.** The board carries no
  pressable and is 2.5 m from the button, outside its 1.6 m radius entirely. The loop sign's near
  edge IS inside that radius (0.57 m), and that is safe for the stronger reason rather than the
  geometric one: it is a bare `Label3D` with no collision and no `pressable` group, so
  `InteractTargeting.Pick` never has it as a candidate. BTN-1's handoff warns about *"a board with
  its own pressable placed inside it"* — neither of these has one.
- **`view.Scores` is the roster.** A spectator with no score row would not appear. Nothing in this
  game produces one today; if a future lane adds spectators, the board is the thing that will not
  know about them.
- **The row labels are four.** A fifth human is counted on the overflow line rather than shown. A
  session that ever wants five is a session that wants a taller panel, not a smaller font.
- **`Run-ReachTest`'s 283-body frame time** (§1.5) is worth someone's attention as a number, not
  as a red.
