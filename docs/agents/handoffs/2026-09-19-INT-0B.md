### 8.1 The two hunks that could not both be true

### 8.2 Why they cannot both be true, in numbers rather than in taste

`RoundClockLayout.ReferenceChars = 7` — how many characters fit across the 2.4 m panel — is not a
guess. CLOCK-1's §3.3 records it being **corrected off a render**: the first cut assumed the
textbook 0.55-of-glyph-height advance and got 8, and the capture of the `Together` line showed
`3 SORTED` (eight characters) hanging off both ends. Measured on that frame the panel spans 275 px
and the eight-character line spans 283, so this font averages 0.64 and the panel holds 7.8
characters. `MinScale = 0.35` is set by the longest line the WIRE can produce,
`HID 255 · SEEK 255` at eighteen characters: 7 / 18 = 0.389, and 0.35 is that with room.

MATCH-1's tally sentence is
`ROUND 1 OF 2 · ADA hid · 3 sorted · found at 1:12 · BEN +48` — **57 characters**. The fit needs
7 / 57 = **0.123** and `MinScale` clamps at 0.35, so the line renders at **2.85× the panel
width**. With a peer whose name has not replicated, `MATCH 1 · PLAYER 1196225384 WINS 255–255` is
about 40 characters and still over twice what the panel can hold. Lowering `MinScale` to fit is
the other direction and is worse: at 0.123 the glyphs are 0.059 m, about 5 px of a 1152-line frame
at 8 m, against the 0.17 m / ~16 px CLOCK-1 measured as the readable floor.

So routing `StripLine` onto the wall either **overflows the panel by ~185 %** or makes the line
**unreadable at the range the clock exists for** — and it turns red the xUnit bound CLOCK-1 wrote
for exactly this, `ClockLine_NeverExceedsWhatTheFitCanRescue`, which is the test that caught its
own copy before any render did.

### 8.3 The disagreement the ruling is right about, and it is real

This is not an argument that the ruling is unnecessary. On the merged tree, at a **match end**,
the strip says `MATCH 1 · BEN WINS 173–171` and the wall still says `HID 1 · SEEK 168` — two
readouts of one card that do not agree about whether a MATCH just ended. In the Holding room after
a match the strip says `MATCH 1 · BEN WON 173–171 · START FOR MATCH 2` and the wall is blank. The
`ROUNDCLOCK-TEST` log above shows the round-1 version of it live:
`clock task TALLY 0:05 shown=HID_3_·_SEEK_168`.

### 8.4 The ruling, and what was built

**FABLE_SUPERMARKET, 2026-09-19 — take the recommendation.** Verbatim:

> Add `HideSeekText.ClockLine(in HideSeekView view, Func<int,string>? nameOf, in HideSeekTuning
> tuning)` that asks MATCH-1's chooser which KIND of sentence is in play (the same
> `card.MatchOver` branch `MatchLine` uses) and renders the clock's own short form, never the
> strip's sentence. Short forms, each must fit `RoundClockLayout`'s rescue bound: match over in
> Tally → `<NAME> WINS` or `DRAW` (names truncated to 6 chars if needed); a round-1 card in
> Tally → today's `HID 3 · SEEK 168`; Holding after a match → `<NAME> WON`; Holding otherwise →
> blank as today; `EndedByDisconnect` → `<NAME> LEFT`. Keep CLOCK-1's `ClockLine` doc sentence
> ("carries one value, never a sentence") and add one line saying the KIND comes from MATCH-1's
> chooser so the wall and the strip cannot disagree about whether a match ended. Unit-test every
> arm against `ClockLine_NeverExceedsWhatTheFitCanRescue`.

Built exactly that, in one commit.

**The chooser is now shared rather than copied.** `MatchLine`'s phase branch was extracted into a
private `ChooseMatchSentence(in HideSeekView)` returning `{None, RoundTally, MatchTally,
MatchHolding}`, and **both** `MatchLine` and the new `ClockLine` overload call it. The phase rules
live in one place; each renderer chooses only how much room it has to say the answer in.
`MatchLine`'s behaviour is unchanged — MATCH-1's forty tests confirm it.

**The arms, as shipped.** `HideSeekText.ClockLine(view, nameOf, tuning)`:

| View | Wall |
|---|---|
| Tally, `MatchOver`, a winner | `BEN WINS` |
| Tally, `MatchOver`, level | `DRAW` |
| Tally, mid-match | `HID 3 · SEEK 168` — CLOCK-1's copy, untouched |
| Holding after a match | `BEN WON` / `DRAW` |
| Holding otherwise, and with no card | blank |
| Either Tally arm, `EndedByDisconnect`, leaver recoverable | `BEN LEFT` |
| ″, leaver **not** recoverable | `ENDED EARLY` — CLOCK-1's word, kept |
| Every other phase | the plain overload, byte for byte |

Three decisions inside that the ruling left to the implementation, each stated rather than
buried:

- **The leaver is recovered exactly as `MatchLine` recovers it** — the card's role holder with no
  row in `view.Scores`, and only when exactly one of the two is missing. When it is not
  recoverable the wall keeps CLOCK-1's `ENDED EARLY`, because naming nobody is worse than a word.
- **Holding after a match keeps the result through a disconnect**, which is MATCH-1's own rule
  for that phase (`MatchHoldingLine`): the match is over with the totals as they stand, and
  hiding the score tells the survivor nothing they do not know. A wall that disagreed with the
  strip about *that* is the same class of defect this overload exists to close.
- **No totals on the wall at a match end.** `BEN WINS`, not `BEN WINS 173–171`. The two numbers
  are on the strip; what the wall is for at that instant is the one word both players look up
  for, and it is the arm that has to carry a name.

`RoundClock.Apply` now takes `(view, nameOf, tuning)` and `RoundAudio`'s poll hands it
`driver.NameOf` / `driver.Tuning` — MATCH-1's rule, not `HideSeekTuning.Current`, so a readout can
never be reading a different object from the one the driver is stepping. CLOCK-1's "carries one
value and never a sentence" paragraph is kept verbatim, with one line under it pointing at the
new overload and saying where the KIND comes from.

**Name truncation is 6 characters**, per the ruling. The panel's bound would allow up to 15 for
the `<NAME> WINS` arm (20 − 5), so 6 is deliberately conservative. One measured artifact worth
knowing: in the probe below both bots were named `MatchEnd*`, so both truncate to `MatchE` and
the wall cannot tell them apart. Real display names do not collide like that and the strip always
carries the full one — but if a playtest ever shows two players sharing a six-character prefix,
the fit bound has room to raise this and nothing else has to move.
