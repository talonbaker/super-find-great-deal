## 1. The gameplay bug, and why one fix closes both findings

SFX-1 shipped a working material palette with a hole in it, measured it, and printed the hole
from its own suite on every run:

```
REMOTE PEER (not the host): 9 prop sound(s) - PickedUp 3, Thrown 6, Impact 0
NOTE: the remote peer heard no IMPACT.
```

**A Loose prop on a non-authority peer is a frozen kinematic body, and Godot reports such a body
no contact at all.** `Carryable.ObservedSpeedMps` (SFX-1) gives that body a speed, which fixed
the *gate*; what it could not fix is that `body_entered` never fires there, so there was nothing
for the gate to judge. No amount of client-side cleverness reaches it, because the client is not
being told.

The second finding is the same shape. CARRY-1's handoff recorded that *"at `ApplyPropState` a
place and a drop are the same Held→Loose transition and are indistinguishable there"*, so SFX-1
had to play the shared `Whoosh` — a sound about the **arm**, not about the object — for a careful
set-down as well as for a throw, and the material's settle tick stayed on `PlaceLooseServer`,
host-only.

Both are one sentence: **the server is the only peer that sees what happened, so the server has
to say it.** Both fixes are the same two lines of architecture — an event the server emits, and a
byte on the state change it already sends.

## 2. The impact event

### 2.1 Where the fire moved from, and why the host's local one had to go too

`Carryable.OnBodyEntered` no longer plays anything for a networked prop. It computes the same
relative-contact-speed intensity it always did, and hands it to `Carryable.ImpactReporter` — an
`Action<float>` that `NetworkedProp` sets next to `OwnedByNetwork`, so this file (the engine-side
body, shared with the offline feel sandbox) keeps knowing nothing about netcode and an offline
`Carryable` keeps its local fire byte-for-byte.

`NetworkedProp.ReportImpact` early-outs on a client. That is the design and not a guard: only the
server simulates a Loose prop, so only the server's copy is ever asked about a contact.

**The host stopped playing its own contact, and that is load-bearing rather than tidy.** If the
local path stayed, the host would play the contact *and* receive its own `CallLocal`
announcement: two sounds ~16 ms apart at the same position. That is a flam, not a louder hit, and
it also spends two voices out of eighteen per collision in exactly the burst where voices are
scarce. One path, one sound — so the two players hear the same thing, which is the whole point of
a shared audio channel (`INTERACTION-BIBLE` §9.3).

The cost is stated rather than hidden: a host's own impact is now heard on the **following
physics tick** rather than inside the contact handler. ~16 ms, against a 0.4 s per-body cooldown
— a factor of twenty-five, and inaudible as timing.

### 2.2 The layout

```csharp
[Rpc(Authority, TransferMode = Unreliable, TransferChannel = NetProfile.PropImpactChannel,
     CallLocal = true)]
private void PropImpact(int propId, int intensityByte, Vector3 position);
```

Three arguments, and each is the smallest thing that answers its question:

- **`propId`** rather than a position lookup, because the receiving peer plays at **its own copy**
  of the prop — the loose stream has already lerped that body to within centimetres, and the node
  is also the particle anchor.
- **`intensityByte`** rather than a float: the value is a 0..1 ramp that ends up driving a volume
  boost and a pitch scale, both of which are already jittered by more than a 1/255 step.
  Quantised **at the source**, before the limiter ranks anything, so the ordering the limiter
  sorts by is exactly the ordering the peers will hear.
- **`position`** as the fallback for a peer whose node is gone.

**The "else play at the sent position" arm the packet describes cannot resolve a sound, and this
is written down rather than quietly skipped.** A peer with no `NetworkedProp` has no
`PresentationProfile` either — the profile is resolved from the prop's collider
(`Carryable.ResolveMaterial`, SFX-1 §2) and lives on the body. Playing *something* generic there
would be a can that sounds like a crate, which is the crossed-wire defect assertion 4 exists to
refuse. The handler logs `[sfx] impact-orphan prop=N at (...)` and plays nothing. Measured in the
runs below: **0 orphans**, which is what you would expect when the spawner replicates every prop
before it can be hit.

### 2.3 Channel 22, unreliable, and the two decisions inside that

**Its own channel, not `PropChannel`.** Channel 4 already carries the reliable ordered
`ApplyPropState` stream (grab, release, settle — the messages a player's hand depends on) *and*
the 30 Hz loose-transform stream. The packet's rule is that a burst of forty impacts must not be
able to delay a grab; sharing the unreliable half would also mean an impact competing with the
very transform updates that describe the collapse it belongs to.

**Unreliable, not reliable-on-its-own-channel**, and this is the half worth arguing. Reliable on
channel 22 would also keep clear of a grab — different channel, no head-of-line blocking — and it
would never lose a sound. It buys that with **order and retransmission**, and for a cosmetic
one-shot those are the wrong currency: a clank from a can that has already stopped rolling is
worse than no clank. `HonkChannel`'s own entry in `NetProfile` states the same rule for the same
reason — *"a beat that arrives late has already missed the moment it was answering."* Nothing here
is state; a lost impact costs one sound and no peer diverges.

**22 was checked against every wave-2 branch on the remote**, not against this lane's base. That
is the discipline INT-0 paid for when three lanes off one base all computed 7896 as "the next
free port": `for b in $(git branch -r) ... git show $b:scripts/net/NetProfile.cs | grep Channel`.
At the time of writing DOOR-1 was the highest at 21 and nothing had taken 22. The ladder comment
now says **next free is 23**.

### 2.4 The limiter

`ImpactBudget.KeepLoudest(pending, 4)` — sorts in place by intensity descending, **prop id
ascending** on a tie, and returns how many survive.

**The tie-break is the load-bearing half and it is not a defensive nicety.** Forty props released
together from the same lattice arrive at the floor with the *same* speed, so the whole burst has
one intensity and "loudest wins" decides nothing at all. `List.Sort` is unstable, so without the
id the survivors would be a different four every run — the shape of bug a suite cannot catch
because it never fails twice the same way. `MECHANICS-BIBLE` §3: *"whichever system happens to run
first is the version that behaves differently on two machines."* Pinned in xUnit by running the
same shuffled burst three times and asserting the same four ids.

Four per tick, and the number comes off the measurement rather than off a feeling: the pool is 18
one-shot voices and an impact recipe runs 0.10–0.40 s, so a *sustained* 4/tick would still
over-subscribe — deliberately. This cap is for the burst. Every prop also carries its own 0.4 s
per-body cooldown, so forty props cannot produce 240 contacts a second between them. What four
buys is that the tick a shelf goes over sends four packets instead of forty, and the four are the
four loudest.

**Flushed at the top of `PropManager._PhysicsProcess`, before the "nothing is Loose" early-out.**
Contacts are reported during the *previous* step's physics flush, and a can that lands and stops
is exactly the case whose clank must not be swallowed because nothing is Loose any more by the
time we get round to sending it.

**And it never engaged on the packet's own fixture — measured, reported, not tuned around.** The
busiest tick of a forty-prop collapse offered **three** contacts against a budget of four. The
per-body 0.4 s cooldown was already bounding that burst; this cap is a backstop for a case the
fixture does not produce. The cap is NOT being lowered to make it bite, because tuning a
constant so a test looks busy is not a measurement. What was added instead is an instrument:
`PropManager` logs its running peak offer (`[sfx] impact-peak offered=N budget=4`), because the
first version printed "never engaged", which reads identically to *the limiter is not wired up*
— the same absence-without-a-positive-control trap this repo's rules file already records for
headless probes. A peak of **zero** is now a hard suite failure.

## 3. The release byte

```csharp
public enum PropRelease : byte { None = 0, Dropped = 1, Placed = 2, Thrown = 3 }
```

It rides `ApplyPropState` as a sixth argument and is stored on `PropState` (a defaulted sixth
positional member, so every existing `new PropState(...)` still compiles). `NetworkedProp.BeginLoose`
— the every-peer half of Held→Loose, the point SFX-1 had already moved the throw fire to — fires
`Thrown` / `Placed` / `Dropped` from it, and **nothing** for `None`.

Who sets what, at the server's funnel:

| Funnel | Byte |
|---|---|
| `RequestPlace` → `PlaceLooseServer` (CARRY-1's place verb) | `Placed` |
| `RequestThrow` → `ReleaseIntoLooseDirected(alongAim: true)` | `Thrown` |
| `RequestDrop` | `Dropped` |
| `ScatterHeldBy` (the burst's forced drop — DOOR-1's door) | `Dropped` |
| `--seed-props-drop` fixture release | `Dropped` |
| every `Resting` broadcast — settle, kill-plane, disconnect, **the round reset** | `None` |
| `SendDumpTo` (late join) | `None`, **unconditionally** |

### 3.1 `None` is a statement, not a default

Two of those rows are the packet's explicit rules and both are asserted:

- **The late-join dump sends `None` whatever the registry stored.** A dump describes a *state*;
  the throw that started a roll happened before this peer existed. Replaying it would be a can
  clanking in an empty aisle the moment you connect.
- **The reset edge is `None`, not `Dropped`.** `HideSeekLoop`'s reset fans through
  `PropManager.ResetForNewPlaythrough`, which returns every prop to its authored transform. That
  is a world rearrangement, not forty people dropping things — and announcing it as forty drops
  would be the loudest lie this system could tell in a game where the other player is navigating
  by what they hear through a wall.

### 3.2 A defect found on the way: `Unbind` was announcing a drop on every peer, always

`NetworkedProp.Unbind` — the every-peer `Resting` latch — reached physics through
`Body.OnDropped()`, which fires `ActorEvent.Dropped`. It discarded that call's toss arc on the
next four lines; it did **not** discard the announcement. So **every settle, every disconnect
release and every reset rehome fanned a "drop" to every peer for every prop involved.**

It was inaudible only because no profile in the repo maps `Dropped` to a sound — luck, not
design, and the day somebody authors one it would have become forty simultaneous thuds at the
round boundary. `Unbind` now calls `Carryable.RejoinPhysicsSilently()` (SFX-1's
`ReleaseAtRestServer`, renamed because the name had stopped being true and it now has two
callers). The physical outcome is identical; the announcement is the release byte's job alone.

`Carryable.OnPlaced` likewise stopped firing `Placed`, for exactly the reason `OnThrown` already
did not fire `Thrown`: it is reached only from `PlaceLooseServer`, which is server-only, so a
tick announced there is a tick only the host hears — and with the byte now firing it on every
peer, firing here as well would give the host two ticks for one placement. Both methods now carry
that reasoning in a `<remarks>` beside each other.

### 3.3 Decoding

`PropReleaseWire.Decode(int)` maps anything outside the four defined ordinals to `None`. Godot RPC
arguments are ints, so the value arriving from another build is an arbitrary int; the one safe
direction for an unknown is the one that plays nothing. Pinned over `-1`, `4`, `255`, `9999`,
`int.MinValue`, `int.MaxValue`.

**`ProtocolVersion` stays 16.** SFX-1 bumped 15 → 16 for this wave's `PropKind` append; the
`ApplyPropState` argument count changing is a data-shape change that would demand a bump on its
own, and one bump carrying both reasons is what the merge needs. Any other wave-2 lane that bumps
must keep **one** bump with all the reasons, not two. Said here because the packet asked for it to
be said.

## 4. `via=`, and the one deviation from the packet

The packet asks for the non-host peer's fires to read **`src=wire`**. They read
**`src=<propId> … via=wire`** instead, and the reason is worth more than the field name.

`src` is `ActorFx`'s context node name, which for a networked prop **is the prop id**, and three
of `Run-MaterialSfxTest`'s assertions are built on that:

1. "each material fired its own voice" — needs to know which prop played;
2. "no crossed wires" — **the assertion `-PlantCrossedProfile` exists to break** — needs to know
   that a tin sound came from a tin prop;
3. the once-per-contact pairing — needs the sound attributed to a body, because two props in
   contact are by definition in the same place at the same moment.

Overwriting `src` with a literal would have destroyed all three to add one bit. SFX-1's own
handoff records losing that attribution once already (*"the first run logged every release as
`src=Props` … a real defect and not only a logging one"*). So the path is its own field,
`ActorFx.ViaLocal` / `ViaWire`, and `src` still names the prop.

`via=wire` on a witness is the proof the packet wants, and it is a **stronger** assertion than
`src=wire` would have been, because assertion 9 can now also fail on `via=local` from a numbered
prop — which is precisely the host double-fire.

### 5.4 Voice budget, before and after

Phase 2, unchanged fixture: forty mixed props on a 5x8 lattice released together, two bots
walking through. The figures are the **windowed bot's** `[sfx] SUMMARY`, which is the same source
SFX-1's table used, so the two rows are directly comparable.

| | SFX-1 (`c3cb295`) | **SFX-2** |
|---|---|---|
| `fires` | 56 | **48** |
| `PeakLive3DVoices` | 18 | **18** |
| `OneShotSteals` | 22 | **10** |
| stolen | **39 %** | **20.8 %** |
| `poolSize` / `ceiling` | 18 / 24 | 18 / 24 (untouched) |

**The improvement is real and the limiter did not cause it. Saying otherwise would be the
easiest false claim in this handoff.** Measured on the same run:

```
IMPACT LIMITER: never engaged. Busiest tick offered 3 contact(s) against a budget of 4
```

**The cap is never reached at the packet's own 40-prop collapse**, and the reason is arithmetic
that was already in the codebase: every prop carries a 0.4 s per-body cooldown, so forty props
can offer at most ~100 contacts a second between them — under two per tick on average, and a
measured peak of three. **The cooldown, not the cap, is what bounds this fixture.** The cap is a
backstop for a case this fixture does not produce (a bigger shelf, or DOOR-1's burst arriving in
lockstep), and xUnit is where it is proved to work.

What actually moved the number is the composition of what the client plays, and it is a
consequence of the **release byte** rather than of the limiter. Counted off the same logs:

| | SFX-1 | SFX-2 |
|---|---|---|
| `Thrown` (Whoosh) on the client | ~40 — one per seeded prop | **0** |
| `Impact` on the client | **0** — the defect | **34** |
| `Step` / `Land` | the remainder | 14 / 2 |

The forty seeded props are released by `--seed-props-drop`, which is a let-go: `PropRelease.Dropped`,
and **no profile in the repo maps `Dropped` to a sound**, so forty simultaneous whooshes became
silence. That is the truer behaviour — nothing threw those props — and it freed exactly the
pressure the client was under. In its place the client now hears 34 impacts it had never heard,
and those arrive *spread out* by the per-body cooldown rather than all on one tick. Fewer fires,
far fewer steals, and the peak still pinned at the pool size.

## 7. Open for the orchestrator

- **The per-tick cap is a backstop that this wave's fixture never reaches** (peak offer 3 of 4).
  If SHELF-1's aisles, or DOOR-1's burst scattering an armful into a shelf, produce a tick with
  more than four contacts, the cap engages and the suite prints the rate. Until then the honest
  statement is that source-side limiting is IMPLEMENTED and PINNED but not yet load-bearing, and
  the 39 % -> 20.8 % steal improvement belongs to the release byte silencing forty spurious
  whooshes. Do not let it be reported as the limiter's.
- **`NetProfile.PropImpactChannel = 22` and the ladder now says next free is 23.** Checked against
  every wave-2 branch on the remote at the time of writing; DOOR-1 was the highest at 21. If
  another lane took 22 in the meantime, this is the INT-0 collision again and one of the two must
  renumber — it is one line here and one line in the ladder comment.
- **`PropManager.Instance` is DOOR-1's member, copied verbatim** (doc comment included, `ServerBurstImpulse`
  cref and all) so the merge collapses to nothing. `PropRegistry.Wake` was **not** needed and is
  not added. If DOOR-1's copy arrives first, delete this one; if this one lands first, DOOR-1's
  diff on that hunk is empty.
- **`ProtocolVersion` is 16 and SFX-2 did not bump it**, on the packet's instruction. The
  `ApplyPropState` argument count changed, which is a data-shape change that would ordinarily
  demand one; the wave's single bump carries it. A lane merging a *second* bump must fold the
  reasons together rather than stack two entries.
- **The impact SQUASH is still host-only.** `Carryable.PunchScale` fires where the contact
  happens, and a remote peer's frozen kinematic body has no contact — so the other player now
  *hears* the can and still does not *see* it react. One line in the wire handler would close it,
  and it is deliberately not in this diff: the packet is about what you hear, and a squash that
  rode the limiter would also disappear for the props whose sound was dropped. Small follow-up.
- **INTERACTION §8.2's redundant channel is unaddressed for prop impacts** (above). Worth a
  design call rather than a packet: in this game a seeker who cannot hear is *supposed* to be
  unable to hear.
- **A release whose `ApplyPropState` is lost loses its sound and nothing else** — it is reliable,
  so it will not be. An **impact** whose event is lost loses its sound entirely, by design
  (unreliable). No counter tracks that today; if playtest ever reports "I didn't hear it", the
  first question is loss on channel 22 and there is nothing to answer it with. A send/receive
  counter pair is cheap if anyone wants it.
- **`Carryable.OnPlaced` and `OnThrown` are now both silent**, and `OnDropped` is the only one of
  the three that still fires — because it is the only one the OFFLINE `CarryController` path
  reaches. Anyone adding a fourth verb should read those three `<remarks>` together before
  choosing where to put its fire.
- **The seeded-drop fixture is now `Dropped` rather than `Thrown`**, so `--seed-props-drop`
  releases are silent under every current profile (nothing maps `Dropped`). SFX-1's suite counted
  6 `Thrown` on the witness; it counts 3 now, one per bot throw, and the gate is `>= 1`. That is
  a truer number, not a regression — nothing threw those four props.
- **`tests/_Common.ps1` still has no single-suite mutex wrapper.** Every lane that runs one suite
  standalone during a live wave writes its own. Worth ten lines in `_Common.ps1` if a third lane
  needs it.
