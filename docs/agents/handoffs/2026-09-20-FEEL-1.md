## 1. The clip, and the one line that caused it

`Carryable.OnPickedUpBySpring` set `CollisionLayer = 0` **and `CollisionMask = 0`**. A held prop
therefore collided with nothing at all — not the holder's capsule, not a shelf — and the carry
spring's steady-state lag (CARRY-1 measured 0.372 m at 3.6 m/s) pulled it straight through the
body whenever the holder moved toward where it was.

The two halves are different questions and they now get different answers:

- **Layer 0** — nothing collides *with* the held prop. The holder walks freely, another player is
  not shoved by what you are carrying, and the place-aim ray still passes through it.
- **Mask 1** — the held prop collides with the *world*. It is frozen kinematic, so nothing is
  simulated; the mask is what lets the sweep ask the broadphase where the body would have stopped.

**The holder's own body is handled by projection, not by collision**, and that was a deliberate
fork: letting a held crate collide with its holder would let it push the player around, which is a
worse bug than the one being fixed.

## 2. The hold, and why nothing snaps

On the grab, the prop's own pose is recorded as three facts and nothing is moved:

1. the **grabbed point** in the prop's own frame (so the object pivots about where you hold it),
2. the **hold distance** along the view ray, clamped to `[HoldMin, HoldMax]`,
3. its **orientation relative to the holder's yaw**.

Feed those three back through `CarryHold` on the next tick and you get the prop's transform
exactly — `CarryHoldTests.OnTheGrabFrame_TheDerivedPoseIsThePropsOwnTransform` pins it.

**Yaw only, never pitch.** Following the yaw keeps the grab's geometry intact as you turn, which
is the clip Talon rode into; not following the pitch is why a thing held in front of you does not
tumble when you look at the floor.

**The grabbed point is kept ON the object** (`CarryHold.GrabPointOnProp`). A bot with no pitch, or
a player looking over a crate on the floor, picks along a ray that misses the prop; without the
clamp the grab offset was **1.2 m of lever**, the hold point orbited that far out, a 180° turn
swept it at 7.5 m/s, and every hold broke 0.3 s after the grab. The log said so in as many words:
`hold broken prop=1014 blocked=1.23m for 0.30s`, nine samples after the grab, every run.

**Four things happen per tick, each a different guarantee** (`NetworkedProp.StepSpring`):

| # | | guarantees |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the target | the grabbed point rides the ray at the hold distance |
| 2 | the spring | CARRY-1's, with `ω` floored at `4v/HoldMin` so the lag stays under half of `HoldMin` |
| 3 | the world | the step is SWEPT, so a crate stops at the shelf face; a sustained block gives the hold up |
| 4 | the holder | the written pose is projected out of the capsule — **the hard guarantee** |

Plus a **speed cap** (`CarryHold.CapStep`, 2× the walk): no response dial and no frame-time spike
can turn a carried crate into a projectile.

**The break-hold rule is about the WORLD, not the spring**, and the difference cost a run. A hold
that is merely lagging — the cap biting through a fast turn, a heavy crate on a slow response — is
the carry working. The clock only runs on a tick where the sweep actually hit something *and* the
prop is past `BreakHoldM` from its target. Without the first half, every grab broke 0.3 s later in
open floor with nothing touching the prop.

## 3. Derived per prop, never a constant

`HoldMin = capsuleRadius + propBoundingRadius + 0.05 m`. At the reference body (capsule 0.36 m):

| prop | bounding radius | HoldMin | HoldMax | notches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| can (35 × 120 mm) | 0.078 m | 0.488 m | 1.200 m | 7 |
| crate (0.44 m cube) | 0.381 m | 0.791 m | 1.200 m | 4 |

The crate is what set the wheel notch: at 0.12 m its band was three and a bit notches, which reads
as a toggle rather than a distance. **0.10 m**, and the unit test is the bar that moved it.

`HoldMin` does two jobs on purpose — the floor of the scroll band, and the clearance the projection
pushes out to — so the two guarantees cannot drift apart.

## 4. The browse pace, and why `MotorTuning.Default` was NOT edited

Talon: *"slow down the players' movement overall; no sprint button; they won't be moving far
enough for it to matter; keep jumping."*

**2.4 m/s**, derived against the level rather than picked off a dial: the search room's aisles are
9.2 m long and its walkways 1.6 m, so 2.4 m/s walks an aisle in 3.8 s and crosses a walkway in two
thirds of a second — fast enough that hunting one wrong object among 130 shelved products is not a
chore, slow enough that you read the shelf you are passing. Every gear declared as a fraction comes
down with it: the walk modifier lands at 1.08 m/s.

**It is applied through `MotorTuning.TryApply` at `Boot._Ready`, not by moving the default, and
this is the one decision in this packet I would defend hardest.** I tried the direct edit first and
measured it: **30 xUnit tests went red**, and they are not bookkeeping — they pin MOVE-8's jump
arc, the skid slide distances, the gear ladder, the cadence bands and the wind curve, all MEASURED
by Talon at a keyboard on 2026-08-28 at 3.8 m/s. Rewriting 30 literals would have deleted that
record rather than slowed this game down, and every one of them would then have been a number
nobody had re-measured. `Current` is what the motor reads and `TryApply` is its documented single
writer — the seam MOVE-4b shipped and MOVE-8 spent. **The supermarket states its own body; the
foundation keeps its.** A future game off this foundation states its own beside it.

`--walk-speed <m/s>` overrides it per process. It is **refused, never clamped**, outside the knob
table's own window: a motor constant that differs between two peers is a desync, so a typo becomes
the shipped default (what the other peers are doing) rather than the nearest legal value.

**Sprint is gone at both ends, and it takes both to be gone rather than hidden**: no binding, the
sampler no longer reads the action or the BT-7 double-tap latch (`Input.IsActionPressed` on a
DELETED action pushes an error every tick — an unbound action and a deleted one are different
things to Godot), and the applied tuning's multiplier is 1.0 so a bot or a doctored client that
sets `MoveIntent.Sprint` gets exactly the walk. The `DoubleTapSprint` type stays: it is pure,
tested, and the next game off this foundation may want it.

### The static-initialiser hazard, stated rather than left

`LocomotionProfile.WalkSpeedMps` / `JogSpeedMps` / `SprintSpeedMps` and the gear hysteresis bands
are `static readonly` snapshots of `AvatarMotor.MoveSpeed`, taken when the type is first touched.
`Boot._Ready` applies the browse pace before any world builds, so in a game process they read 2.4;
in `dotnet test` nothing calls `ApplyAtBoot`, so they read the foundation's 3.8 and every gear test
is unaffected. **It works by ordering rather than by construction.** Nothing observable depends on
it today (at 2.4 m/s a body classifies as Jog under either set of bands), but a lane that starts
reading those statics earlier should make them properties rather than fields.

## 5. Every peer agrees, and it costs no wire

F5 offered a `ProtocolVersion` bump to carry the grab offset. **It is not needed, and the reason is
that the wire already carried the answer:** `ApplyPropState`'s `Held` broadcast has always included
the prop's transform at the moment of the grab, and the `Held` arm **discarded it**. Every
non-holder peer now records that pose *relative to the holder's body* and replays it against
wherever that body is now — so a spectator sees the object where the holder is actually carrying
it, tracking their turn, instead of welded to a chest socket.

**Protocol version stays at 16.** No field was added, no argument count moved, no message widened.

Two documented mismatches remain, both the same class as the hold ROTATION (local since CARRY-1,
and carried to everyone only by the transform a PLACE sends): a non-holder sees the **grab-time**
hold distance rather than the wheel's current one, and the spring's lag. A peer's view is projected
out of the holder's capsule too, because a crate inside the hider's chest is exactly as bad on the
seeker's screen as on their own — the suite asserts both views.

## 6. Click to interact — the audit Talon asked for

`interact` moved from `E` to the left mouse button; the joypad keeps button 2; the action itself is
untouched, so every bot and `Run-ButtonsTest` (which go through RPCs) are unaffected.

**Every `interact` consumer in the tree, audited:**

| consumer | reached by | one LMB click does it |
|---|---|---|
| Carry grab | `SandboxAvatar.HandleCarryIntent` → `ClientRequestGrab` | yes |
| Carry release | same → `ClientRequestPlace` | yes |
| The round's three buttons (Start / Confirm / End) | `RoundButton` (`IPressable`) → `InteractHighlighter` arms `InteractOverride` → the same edge | yes — all three, by one binding |
| The offline feel lab | `FeelSandboxWorld.InteractOverride` | yes (not shipped in the game) |

There is nothing else. HOLD-1's practice pad is an `IPlacementValidator`, not a press;
`BurstDoor` is event-driven off the round, not player-pressed; the pause and How-to-Play panels use
`pause` and the UI actions. **No door, toggle or plate in this build reads `interact` and was
missed** — and any future one plugs into `IPressable` / `WorldInteract` and inherits the button.

`InteractPrompt` now resolves its chip label through `ControlGlyphs.BindingFor`, which understands
a mouse button. It used to walk the event list itself, skip everything that was not a key, and fall
back to the literal `"E"` — so every world chip would have gone on advertising a dead key.

## 7. No physics freakouts — what is guaranteed, and PHYS-1's first item

The held prop's per-tick step is capped at 2× the walk speed, so the energy a carry can deposit is
bounded whatever the response dial says. Measured over the suite's run: **fastest never-held prop
0.00 m/s** against Talon's 3 m/s bar, and **nothing flung** (no never-held prop moved more than 2 m
from where it started).

**And the honest half, which is PHYS-1's:** 0.00 m/s is not evidence that the cap works, because
**nothing moved at all**. A Resting prop is frozen kinematic on **every** peer including the server
(`NetworkedProp._Ready`, `Unbind`), so a held crate driven into a stack of boxes transfers nothing —
the sweep stops the crate at their face and the boxes do not so much as rock. The suite reports the
count (`PHYS1EVIDENCE movedRestingProps=`) rather than gating on it, exactly as the orchestrator
directed.

> **PHYS-1's first item, with the evidence.** Resting props are `Freeze = true`,
> `FreezeMode = Kinematic` on every peer; only a prop the server has explicitly released into
> `Loose` simulates at all. Until something wakes a resting body on contact, "they should fall over
> like dominoes, cans should roll" cannot happen, and no carry-side change can make it. The bar to
> keep when it does: **no woken prop above 3 m/s, none outside RoomBounds** — this suite already
> measures both and will start meaning something the day the bodies wake.

## 8. Deviations from the packet, each with its reason

1. **No `ProtocolVersion` bump (F5).** The wire already carried the grab-time transform; see §5.
2. **The grab offset is derived locally rather than sent (F5).** The holder computes it from its
   own view at bind time, which needs no caller change and no new field, and gives the same no-snap
   property for a bot with no camera.
3. **A click while holding always PLACES; the place-vs-drop branch is gone (F1).** The branch
   existed because a held prop could be left hanging inside a shelf; it cannot be any more. `Q`
   still throws, and drop is still what a disconnect and a round reset use.
4. **The server's place reach moved from the chest anchor to the EYE**, with the allowance derived
   from the hold's own ceiling. Not doing this would have refused an honest placement — a player
   looking down to set a can on the floor at their feet was `TooFarToPlace`.
5. **The wheel no longer rotates a held object.** `HeldPropRotator`'s coarse 15° wheel steps are
   removed so the wheel has one meaning; RMB + mouse is untouched, which is the gesture Talon said
   he liked.
6. **The item's authored hold rotation is no longer applied.** It exists to seat a prop in a socket
   at a chosen angle, and the ruling is that the object keeps the orientation it was grabbed at.
7. **`MotorTuning.Default` was not edited** — §4.
8. **udp/7913, not 7912** — a live collision with a SOLO-1 lane; see the rules-file entry.
9. **One SICK-1 file touched, one hunk**: `SandboxAvatar`'s intent-source setter now respects
   `SuppliesLook`, and `HoldDistanceController.Attach` is one line beside `HeldPropRotator.Attach`.
   Neither is in the look path SICK-1 owns; `FirstPersonCamera.cs` and `project.godot`'s
   display/physics settings were not touched at all. The `[input]` block is mine by the packet.

**Run-CarryHoldTest, udp/7913** (machine: an idle foreign headless server of Talon's was alive on
the box; no suite contended):

```

witness  clearance: worst=0.0010m mean=0.0552m over 49 held sample(s), 0 tick(s) inside the holder

freakout:            fastest never-held prop 0.00 m/s (bar 3); 0 flung
```

The holder's worst clearance being **exactly `HolderSkinM`** is the projection doing its job: the
stress loop does drive the prop onto the shell, and the shell is where it stops.

**Proved able to fail.** `CollisionMask = 0` planted back into `OnPickedUpBySpring`:

| | penetration into the pillar | clip bars |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | **0.001 m** | 0 ticks inside, both views |
| planted | **0.214 m** (the crate half inside a 1 m pillar) | **still 0 ticks inside, both views** |

The second column is the useful half: the capsule projection and the world sweep are independent
guarantees, so one plant cannot flatter both, and a red says which mechanism moved.

**The lag table**, `Run-CarryHoldTest`'s own samples, a 0.44 m crate at the 2.4 m/s browse pace:

| | mean | peak | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| before (CARRY-1's measurement, 3.6 m/s walk, chest anchor) | 0.372 m | 0.403 m | 83 |
| **after (ray hold, 2.4 m/s, stress loop incl. two 180s per cycle)** | **0.223 m** | **0.809 m** | 49 |

The two are not the same experiment and the peak says so: CARRY-1's was a straight walk, this is a
loop whose whole purpose is the manoeuvre a straight walk cannot stage. The **peak is the 180s** —
the hold point orbits the holder at the hold distance and sweeps faster than the speed cap allows,
so the prop trails and then catches up, which is what a heavy object does when you spin. The
steady-state number to compare with 0.372 m is the mean.

`Run-CarryDriftTest`, unchanged and green: **mean 0.894 m, peak 0.902 m, growth 0.001 m, n = 83**
(was 1.010 / 1.063 / 0.000). The caps were not touched — the number went DOWN, because the armful
lift that raised a crate above the carry mount went with the socket. See the rules-file entry.

## 10b. What the first marathon found — four suites, and only one of them was a flake

The first full marathon went **44 suites, 40 PASS / 4 FAIL**, with the new suite green. Three of
the four reds were carry suites, which is exactly where this diff lands, so none of them was
allowed to be "the known flake" without a measurement. **One was contention, one was a latent bug
this packet made load-bearing, and two were bars this packet invalidated.**

### `Netcode: replication` — contention, not a red

`Couldn't create an ENet host` on **udp/7777** — another worktree's live Godot held it. FP-1's
handoff records the same collision on the same port and the same verdict. Nothing in this diff
touches replication.

### `Carry: regrab-while-loose` — a REAL bug, and it is not the one the flake list names

**3/3 red standalone on an idle machine**, which is already the opposite of the documented
signature (that flake is 3/3 PASS standalone, recorded four times). Two causes, stacked:

1. **The suite's throw scale is a RATIO whose denominator moved.** Its own table is written
   against *"the bot walks ~3.6 m/s"* and picks 0.35 so the ball is *"slower than the bot from
   the first frame"*. At the 2.4 m/s browse pace, 0.35 × 7.5 = 2.6 m/s is FASTER than the bot, and
   the race the table exists to abolish came back: the bot was stranded at (7.28, 7.17) while the
   ball rolled to x = 10.59 and was still rolling at the end. Re-derived to **0.23**, keeping the
   table's own ratio (0.35 × 2.4/3.6) rather than picking a new number.
2. **The Held broadcast has always sent the wrong transform, and this packet made it matter.**
   `PropManager` sent `node.GlobalTransform` — the NetworkedProp NODE, which is placed once at
   spawn and never moves again. The physical `Carryable` child is what travels, which is why
   every other line in that file reads `WorldPosition => Body.GlobalPosition`. **The Held arm of
   `ApplyPropState` discarded the argument entirely**, so the staleness was invisible for as long
   as it existed. FEEL-1 makes it the pose every non-holder carries the prop at — and a witness
   watching a regrab saw the ball teleport to its SPAWN corner on the grab and trail its holder by
   **5.52 m** for the rest of the run. Now `node.Body.GlobalTransform`, at both Held broadcasts.

**That is the most valuable thing this packet found**, and it found it by making a discarded value
load-bearing. 3/3 PASS standalone afterwards.

### `Carry: networked proof` — a bar this packet invalidated, twice

First red: *"held prop 2 sat 1.61 m from its holder (> 1.6 m)"*, 110 times. `$HoldRadius = 1.6`
was a typed number reused from `Run-RegrabTest` and correct for a prop on a chest socket 0.88 m
from the body. A prop on the view ray sits up to 1.2 m from an eye 0.995 m up — a geometric
maximum of **1.56 m** before any lag. Re-derived to **2.0 m** with the arithmetic written at the
constant.

Second red, after that: *"held prop 1 sat 33.22 m from its holder"*, falling by about a metre a
sample. **A hold must not EASE across a room.** The round teleports a holder between rooms, the
target moves 40 m in one tick, and the spring plus the speed cap then walked the crate across the
level at 4.8 m/s. `NetworkedProp.ShouldSeat` seats the hold outright when the target is further
away than any honest lag could put it (`HoldMax + BreakHoldM` = 1.8 m, against a measured worst
honest lag of 0.809 m). PASS afterwards.

### `Carry: place + integrity` — the break-hold rule, and a number the dressed room moved

Bots C and D lost their crates before their scripted places fired, and three things came out of it:

- **The break now asks for a DROP, not a PLACE.** A place can be refused — the prop is wedged in
  whatever stopped it, so integrity answers `Overlapping` — which leaves the player holding
  something the game has decided they lost, and spends the refusal channel the HUD and three
  suites read on an event nobody asked for. A drop cannot be refused, which is the right property
  for a transition the world forced.
- **`BreakHoldSec` is 0.8 s, not F4's 0.3 s, and the reason is STOCK-1's room.** A bot carrying a
  crate eleven metres down the z = −2.1 walkway — an ordinary journey, not a shove — had the hold
  broken **three times** by brushing floor bins and pallet stacks it was walking past, and arrived
  empty-handed. **This is the number to watch on Talon's ride**: losing the object because you
  clipped a bin is worse than the defect the rule prevents. It is a value call and it is stated at
  the constant rather than smuggled.
- **The suite's own staging broke HOLD-1's rule** — *"walk a bot along its own walkway, never
  diagonally"* — which had no teeth until a held prop could collide. Bot D now walks its own
  walkway and carries the crate tucked in close (`--hold-notches`, through the same `ScrollHold`
  the wheel calls). Its refusal target is unchanged, because the target is what the phase asserts.
