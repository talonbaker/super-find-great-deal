The local human plays in first person. Mouse look drives yaw and pitch, WASD moves relative to the
look yaw, the camera sits on the character's own measured eyeline, the player's body is not drawn
into its own eyes, and the third-person rig is no longer attached for humans. **There is no toggle
back**, deliberately: a game whose whole subject is what you can and cannot see from where you are
standing cannot have two answers to "where are the eyes".

## The yaw decision, and why it is a knob

**In first person the body follows the LOOK, not the travel direction.** `MotorTuning` knob 58,
`BodyYawFollowsAim`, ships at 1.

The reasoning is the packet's own and it is a design fact before it is a technical one: a player's
body is the only thing the *other* player can read, and a body that faces its travel direction
tells them where you are walking while you stare at the shelf you are about to hide something in.
It is also the only way the carry anchor, the aim ray and the rendered view can agree about which
way "in front of me" is — `AvatarProportions.CarryAnchorRestLocal` is expressed in the BODY's
local space, so with travel-facing a first-person player would carry the object out of shot every
time they strafed.

**It is a mode on an existing seam, not a branch.** `AvatarMotor.Step` already took an optional
`faceYaw` (built for a swing verb that did not come over; nothing passed it). At 1 the knob is what
supplies it, from the intent's own already-sanitized `MoveIntent.AimYaw`:

```csharp
float? aimedFacing = faceYaw ?? (BodyYawFollowsAim ? SanitizeAimYaw(intent.AimYaw) : (float?)null);
```

So: **nothing new crosses the wire** (`AimYaw` has ridden the input packet since v8 and is already
replayed in reconciliation), the authority, the owner's prediction and the replay all derive the
same facing from the same four bytes, and `ResolveYaw` itself is untouched — its existing tests
still hand it an explicit angle. `PredictionMatchTests` is unchanged and green. An explicit
`faceYaw` from a caller still beats the knob, because it is derived from the same aim *plus*
something the authority owns.

**With the knob on, an aimed facing also beats the skid/slide clause.** That is correct for this
game: a player looking somewhere on purpose while they slide does not want the body — and the
carry anchor hanging off it — swinging round to face the drift.

### What it costs, and what pays it

A scripted brain carries no look, so it reports `AimYaw = 0`; at knob 1 every bot and dummy in the
repo would pivot to world zero and walk sideways through every capture a later packet takes.
**`TravelFacingIntentSource`** is the answer: a decorator that leaves every field alone and fills
`AimYaw` from that same intent's own `MoveDir`, holding the last heading while the brain stands
still, using `ResolveYaw`'s own travel-facing arithmetic. So a fixture faces exactly where it faced
before the knob existed — measured, not assumed: `Run-MultiplayerTest -BotCount 2` reports
`bot1 path 3.33m, bot2 path 3.33m, max position error 0.000m`, which is BASE-1's line to the
centimetre.

It is applied in **one place** — `SandboxAvatar.IntentSource`'s setter, which is the single point
any source is adopted (five construction sites: the networked bot branch, the human branch, the
offline sandbox player and dummy, and `SandboxSelfTest`'s brain). The next scripted brain someone
writes inherits it without having to know the rule exists. A human source is passed through
untouched; the decorator forwards `IsHumanInput` rather than defaulting it, and
`BotAchievementGateTests` asserts that on an instance rather than trusting the declaration.

It also fixes a latent lie: `AimYaw` is what the SERVER rebuilds an aim ray from, and a bot that
reported zero was claiming to look due north no matter which way it walked. Nothing reads that
today; the first server-side verb to do so would have been bitten by it.

|---|---|---|---|
| `MotorTuning.BodyYawFollowsAim` | **1** (on) | `scripts/net/MotorTuning.cs`, row 58 | Above. 0 restores the foundation's travel-facing rule; both behaviours run through `ResolveYaw` |
| `MotorTuning.AimTurnLerp` | 22 s⁻¹ (**unchanged**) | already shipped | The rate the body swings onto the look — about 83 ms to close the angle at 60 Hz. Not touched: it is a signed-off feel number and the knob above is what made it live |
| `FirstPersonCamera.DefaultFovDeg` | **75** | `FirstPersonCamera.cs` | The third-person rig's own resting lens and the conventional first-person default. Wider reads as speed, which is exactly the channel this rig may not use |
| `FirstPersonCamera.NearPlaneM` | **0.05** | same | The lens sits inside the player's own collision capsule; anything further out clips through a wall the body is pressed against before the body stops |
| `SandboxCamera.MouseSensitivity` | 0.0025 rad/px (**unchanged**) | `SandboxCamera.cs`, now `public` | Read, not copied, so a future sensitivity slider has one number to move |
| `SandboxCamera.PitchMin`/`PitchMax` | ∓1.05 rad (**unchanged**) | `SandboxCamera.cs` | Shared with `AvatarMotor.SanitizeAimPitch`, so a legitimate look can never be an illegal aim |
| `AvatarVisual.FirstPersonHiddenLayer` | **19** | `AvatarVisual.cs` | See below |

**Deliberately absent, and this is the motion-sickness half of the packet:** no head bob, no camera
lag or follow spring, no speed-FOV, no landing dip, no depth of field, and no gamepad stick path
(not required). None of them is a knob set to zero — none of them is built into the class. A
third-person rig can afford those because the lens is a camera looking *at* a character; in first
person the lens is the character's head, and every one of them is a movement the player's inner ear
did not ask for. `SandboxCamera`'s own landing dip (`CameraDipStrengthM`) is untouched and still
ships at its exact no-op 0.00 for the rigs that keep it.

## Hiding the body: layer 19, and why not layer 20

`AvatarVisual.OwnBodyRenderLayer` = 20 already existed, and reusing it was wrong in two ways.

1. **Layer 20 is carried by EVERY avatar, on every peer.** The third-person rig's use of it is
   momentary — the spring arm drops the layer for the handful of frames it would otherwise render
   from inside a head, and it does not matter that every other body blinks with it. A first-person
   cull is PERMANENT, so a lens that dropped 20 would erase **the other player**, who in this game
   is the whole thing you are looking for.
2. **Layer 20 does not cover the whole body.** Measured, not reasoned — fault plant 1 below printed
   the layer of every mesh on the rig: the ten body meshes were on `0x80000` (layer 20) and
   `ThighL/ShinL/FootL/ThighR/ShinR/FootR` were on `0x1` (layer 1). `MarkOwnBodyLayer` walks
   `_body`, and the legs and feet hang off the rig root instead. A first-person player would have
   looked down at six floating leg segments.

So: `FirstPersonHiddenLayer` = 19, set on **one avatar per process** (the one this machine drives),
covering the **whole** `AvatarVisual` subtree — body, feet, sprout, and the two lazily-built parts
(the frozen block and the knocked-out birds, which are built long after the camera attaches and are
re-hidden on creation). Re-applied after an appearance rebuild, because a networked spawn rebuilds
its model when the authority's avatar-key pick lands — *after* the camera attached — and a rebuilt
mesh comes back on the default layer with the player suddenly inside their own head.

**A cull mask, not a hidden node and not a zero scale.** Visibility and scale are properties of the
node, so both would travel to every peer's copy and to the spectate camera; a cull mask is a
property of the LENS. The body keeps existing, keeps animating, keeps casting its blob shadow, and
is still drawn — with its nameplate — by every camera in every other process.

**The blob shadow is deliberately NOT hidden.** It is not part of the visual rig (it is a top-level
quad on the floor under the body) and it is the one ground-contact cue a first-person player has
left once their legs are gone. Every first-person game draws the player's own shadow; none draws
the inside of their own skull.

## The nameplate: packet item 5 said "verify", and the answer was no

The packet said the local player's nameplate "already isn't" drawn to itself. **It is not already
safe, and this was measured rather than reasoned.**

`UpdateNameplate` projects a point ~0.46 m directly above the lens. With the look level that point
lands on the near plane and `Camera3D.IsPositionBehind` hides it **by accident** — which is exactly
what "it already isn't drawn" looks like. Past roughly five degrees of up-look the anchor crosses in
front of the lens and the player's own name is projected across the top of their own screen.

It is now suppressed at the source (`SandboxAvatar.IsLocalFirstPersonBody`, written only by
`FirstPersonCamera.Attach`). Fault plant 2 below removed the suppression and the probe went red
**with both of its positive controls passing** — i.e. the anchor genuinely was in shot and world UI
genuinely was not suppressed for some other reason. That is the measurement.

## `SandboxCamera` is still in the tree, and here is who needs it

Not attached for any human. It is kept for:

- **`--spectate-cam`** — the orbit camera a windowed bot gets for marketing/framing captures where
  the subject is the avatar rather than its view. Untouched.
- **The offline dev harnesses** — `SandboxWorld` and `feel/FeelSandboxWorld`, which build a
  `SandboxCamera` plus `LocalInputIntentSource`. `Run-SandboxTest` (121/121) and `Run-FeelTest` are
  green on it.
- **Its shared constants** — `PitchMin`/`PitchMax` and `MouseSensitivity`, which the first-person
  rig reads rather than duplicating.

`LocalInputIntentSource` is likewise kept and is now third-person-only. It and
`FirstPersonIntentSource` are siblings that share **one** sampler (`HumanInputSampler`, which is
the old `NextIntent` body verbatim): they differ in which `ILookAngles` supplies the yaw and in
nothing else, so a new input verb lands in one place rather than two.

## The bible check

```
Bibles applied:  INTERACTION (the player acts through this: it decides what they can see and
                 what E resolves against), MECHANICS §2.5 (the facing change is a state change
                 other players read off your body). LEVEL does not apply — this arranges
                 nothing in space; it changes where the eyes are in a level BASE-1 authored.
Items checked:   INTERACTION §1 affordance, §2 feedback, §3 physical/visual correspondence,
                 §4 range & timing, §7 interrupt handling; MECHANICS §2.5.
Result:          pass, with two things fixed to make it pass — see below.
```

- **§1 affordance — "hidden is a real intent, worth saying so."** The local body is hidden on
  purpose; it is said so in `FirstPersonHiddenLayer`'s own doc, in `HideOwnBodyFromFirstPerson`, and
  measured by the smoke rather than left to a reviewer's eye.
- **§2 feedback — "at the exact moment of the trigger."** Mouse look is applied in
  `_UnhandledInput` and the basis is written every RENDER frame, not every physics tick, so the view
  moves at the rate the player's screen does. The lens is a child of the body, so it cannot lag the
  collider by a frame — which is what a follow spring on a first-person camera would be.
- **§3 physical/visual correspondence — this is the yaw decision.** "State changing internally with
  nothing moving is the shortcut that later gets reported as it didn't work": a first-person player
  who turns to look at something and whose body does not turn is exactly that, to the *other*
  player. Knob 58 is the fix and `MECHANICS §2.5`'s multiplayer generalisation is why it is on.
- **§4 range & timing — "worth pinning rather than inheriting."** `AimCamera` is repointed at the
  first-person lens in the same method that builds it, so `InteractTargeting.Pick`'s aim cone is now
  a real first-person cone rather than an orbit camera's. **Pinned rather than inherited**: had this
  been missed, E would have kept acting on whatever the third-person lens used to see, silently.
  BTN-1 gets this for free and should not re-derive it.
- **§7 interrupt handling — "the failure mode is the player stuck in a state neither can leave."**
  The pause overlay's capture/release is untouched and still global (`PauseOverlay` saves and
  restores `Input.MouseMode`); `HumanInputSampler` returns `MoveIntent.None` **and** clears the
  analog ramp and the double-tap sprint latch whenever the cursor is free, so a menu cannot strand a
  player sprinting. `FirstPersonCamera` refuses mouse motion while the cursor is visible, exactly as
  `SandboxCamera` does.
- **Fixed to make it pass:** the local nameplate (INTERACTION §1 — a label the player cannot act on
  drawn across their own view is anti-affordance), and the leg/foot meshes that layer 20 did not
  cover (§1 again: six floating shins is the opposite of a deliberate absence).

## Deviations from the packet, and why

1. **The full scene marathon (`Run-AllTests.ps1`) was not run.** The packet's own Gate list does not
   include it, and the program says so twice: §0 "light R&D gates per lane (xUnit + one smoke +
   captures)" and §3, which assigns the full scene suite to INT-1. Two sibling lanes (ROUND-1,
   CARRY-1) were live in their own worktrees and a marathon holds the machine-wide mutex for the
   better part of an hour, which would have starved both. Instead: the four suites this diff can
   reach were run standalone (above), and the new suite is **registered** in `Run-AllTests.ps1` so
   INT-1's marathon picks it up. **INT-1 should note that the marathon now needs a desktop
   session.**

2. **`--fp-look` was added; the packet did not ask for it.** A capture harness for a mouse-look game
   has to be able to aim. A bot brain decides where it WALKS, and in first person that no longer
   decides where it LOOKS, so without this every first-person capture in the repo is pointed
   wherever yaw zero happens to face. It is what puts two bodies in front of each other's lenses
   for the evidence above, and CARRY-1, SHELF-1 and DOOR-1 will each want it. View-only: it sets
   the camera, never the intent.

3. **The smoke's windowed client is a `--bot`, not a human client.** A non-bot client needs a human
   to press Join → Direct Connect; nothing in `tests/` can drive that. `--first-person-cam` makes a
   bot render through the *real* rig (one shared `AttachFirstPersonCamera`), so the suite measures
   the camera a player looks through rather than a second one built to resemble it.

4. **`TravelFacingIntentSource` touches every scripted brain, which the packet's "bots … are
   untouched" could be read as forbidding.** It is the opposite: *not* wrapping them is what would
   have changed bot behaviour, because knob 58 would have pivoted every one of them to world zero.
   The decorator reproduces their previous facing exactly, and `Run-MultiplayerTest`'s numbers are
   BASE-1's to the centimetre.

5. **The blob shadow is not hidden** (reasoning above), so "the local player's own body is not
   drawn" is precisely true of the `AvatarVisual` rig and not of the quad on the floor under it.

## For CARRY-1 and BTN-1 specifically

- **The aim ray is unchanged.** `MoveIntent.AimYaw`/`AimPitch` are still the source of truth and are
  still what the server rebuilds the ray from (`AimQuery.DirectionFromYawPitch`). Nothing about
  first person changes the seam CARRY-1 was told it does not depend on.
- **`SandboxAvatar.AimCamera` is the first-person lens** (`FirstPersonCamera.CameraNode`), for
  client-side `InteractTargeting.Pick`. If you build a new camera path, set it in the same method
  that builds the camera; that coupling is what keeps E acting on what the player is looking at.
- **The cull layer is 19** (`AvatarVisual.FirstPersonHiddenLayer`). **A carried prop must NOT be put
  on it** — the whole point of the R.E.P.O. carry is seeing the thing in your hands. Props are on
  layer 1 and should stay there; only the holder's own body leaves the mask.
- **The carry anchor now sits in front of where the player is LOOKING**, because the body yaw
  follows the look (knob 58). That is what CARRY-1's spring wants, and it is why the knob defaults
  on rather than being left to a later packet.
- **`--fp-look <yawDeg>[,<pitchDeg>]`** aims a `--first-person-cam` bot's lens. Use it for any
  first-person capture; without it your shot is pointed at whatever yaw zero faces.
- **The body lags the look by about 83 ms** (`AimTurnLerp` 22 s⁻¹). If a placement verb needs the
  body settled before it commits, that is the number.
