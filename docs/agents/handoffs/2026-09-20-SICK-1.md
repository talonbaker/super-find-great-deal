## 0. The diagnosis, in three sentences

**The first-person lens's world position was never computed for the frame that was being drawn.**
It is a child of the avatar and took its position from parentage, so it was whatever the last
60 Hz physics tick happened to leave on the raw `CharacterBody3D` — which meant (1) every
reconciliation correction landed on the player's eyes **one for one**, measured at corrections of
0.206 / 0.356 / 0.243 / 0.197 m producing eye steps of **0.190 / 0.380 / 0.242 / 0.190 m**, and on
a cold join, seven eye teleports of **1.20–1.65 m** inside the first three seconds, each on a
normal 17 ms frame; and (2) at any render rate that is not exactly 60 Hz the view was frozen for
most frames and jumped a whole tick on the rest, measured at **96.3 %** of frames frozen windowed
with vsync off (~2 100 fps) and **58.5 %** on the headless control at 145 fps, against the
**58.6 %** that `1 − 60/R` predicts. Both are the same mechanism and both go to zero when the eye
is interpolated between the last two physics samples of `RenderGlobalPosition`: `--cam-interp 1`
takes the frozen-frame share from **96.3 % to 0.0 %** and turns a **1.504 m** correction into a
**0.118 m** step.

**What this means for the ride.** Talon's viewpoint was being teleported over a metre, sideways
and backwards, while his own body, his own shadow, his own nameplate and the object in his hands
all moved smoothly — the classic inner-ear conflict, delivered by the one thing in the frame he
could not look away from.

## 1. Reading before measuring: the ranked suspects, and what happened to each

The packet's method, followed in order. **Every writer of the camera transform was read first**
and the list is short: `FirstPersonCamera.Attach`, `SetLook`, `_Process` → `ApplyLookBasis`,
`AdvanceKick` (DOOR-1's kick), and — the one nobody had written down — **parentage**.

| # | Suspect, from reading | Verdict | The number that decided it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | No physics interpolation; the camera follows a 60 Hz body at the screen's rate | **CONFIRMED, and it is the dominant term at any refresh but 60** | frozen-frame share **96.3 %** at ~2 100 fps, **58.5 %** at 145 fps (predicted 58.6 %), **0.8 %** at 60 fps |
| **2** | The lens is the one per-frame tracker in the repo NOT reading `SandboxAvatar.RenderGlobalPosition` | **CONFIRMED, and it is the dominant term at 60 Hz** | correction → eye step, one for one; 1.20–1.65 m jumps on a cold join |
| 3 | `DefaultFovDeg = 75` is "narrow on a 16:9 desktop monitor" | **KILLED — the premise is an arithmetic error** | 75 is the VERTICAL angle; read back from the engine, `keep_aspect=Height`, **107.5° horizontal** at 1280×720 |
| 4 | Mouse look applied per event rather than accumulated per frame | **KILLED by reading and then by measurement** | the basis is written ONCE per render frame; yaw-step SD under a constant 45 °/s turn is **0.570° before / 0.559° after**, i.e. unmoved |
| 5 | No static visual reference at all (own body culled, no crosshair) | **Real, not fixed here** | §6 |
| 6 | Movement inertia | **Real, not fixed here — another lane owns the motor** | `MoveSpeed / Acceleration` = 3.8 / 9 = **0.42 s** to reach walking speed, against the packet's 0.15 s bar |
| 7 | The held object bobs in the near field while the camera is rock steady | **Real, FEEL-1 owns the files** | §6 |
| 8 | A HUD `Control` swallowing mouse motion before `_UnhandledInput` | **KILLED by reading** | every `Control` the HUD builds is `MouseFilterEnum.Ignore` |

**Suspect 2 is the one no reading list would have produced from the packet**, and it came out of
`RenderGlobalPosition`'s own doc comment, which says in as many words: *"Everything cosmetic that
tracks the avatar per frame — camera focus, carry anchor, blob shadow — must read this, not
`GlobalPosition`, or it visibly detaches from the rendered mesh under real network conditions."*
`SandboxCamera` reads it. `BlobShadow` reads it. `UpdateNameplate` reads it. `Carryable` reads it.
`FirstPersonCamera` did not — and **FP-1's own header argued that parentage was a virtue**
("the camera inherits the body's position for free and can never lag it by a frame, which is what
a follow spring on a first-person lens would be"). That is true, and it is also exactly how the
defect got in: the thing parentage hands you for free is the body **before** the render layer has
hidden the correction.

## 2. The instrument, and why it is not PROBE-1's

PROBE-1's `BotHarness` reading (`prs`/`fms`/`draws`/`objs`, `feat/2026-09-20-probe-1` @ `71f7606`)
is a **5 Hz spot sample** whose `fms` is derived from `TimeFps`, which is an **average**. Both
choices are right for the question PROBE-1 asked — what does N props cost — and both destroy this
one: judder is a property of the distribution of CONSECUTIVE frames, and a run whose average frame
time is a flawless 6.1 ms can be alternating 3 ms and 9 ms.

**So the four PROBE-1 fields were COPIED, not cherry-picked**, and this handoff says so because the
packet asked which. They are three lines of `Performance.GetMonitor`; that commit also carries
`ProbeSeedLayout`, `PropManager`'s seeding, `ReachCostProbe` and `Probe-Capacity.ps1`, none of
which this lane touches and all of which sit in files other lanes were live in. They appear in
`CameraPacingProbe`'s summary line as a spot reading taken once, at the end of the window, so a
pacing row can be read next to a draw-call count — never as a second pacing measurement.

| Thing | Where |
|---|---|
| The pure maths (eye between two ticks; run → summary) | `scripts/game/sandbox/CameraPacing.cs`, engine-free, xUnit |
| The per-frame recorder | `scripts/game/sandbox/CameraPacingProbe.cs`, built only when `--pacing-log` is given |
| A bot that is still walking at 30 s | `ScriptedPatrolIntentSource` in `scripts/game/sandbox/IntentSources.cs`, `--goto-patrol x1,z1,x2,z2` |
| The driver | `tests/Measure-CameraPacing.ps1`, **unregistered**, **udp/7914** |
| The raw data | `docs/qa/2026-09-20-sick-1/*.csv` — one row per render frame |

**`--goto-patrol` exists because every other scripted brain in the repo was disqualified.**
`ScriptedGotoIntentSource` latches on arrival and stops; `ScriptedCarryIntentSource`'s patrol lives
inside its hold phase, so using it would have put a held object in the near field — and a held
object in the near field is one of the things under investigation, so a measurement rig may not
smuggle one in. It steers off the PREDICTED position and takes no irreversible action at all,
which is the distinction `ScriptedGotoIntentSource`'s own header draws between itself and the carry
source, and the wording of the W6-3 rule ("must not take an irreversible ACTION on one").

## 3. The before/after table

Windowed 1280×720, one headless server plus one `--first-person-cam` bot patrolling a clear lane of
the holding room while the probe turns the look at a constant 45 °/s. 30 s per row. **This machine's
display reports 60.0 Hz** (`DisplayServer.ScreenGetRefreshRate`), and that fact is load-bearing —
see the note under the table.

### 3.1 The headline: the frozen-frame share

| Row | `--cam-interp` | vsync | dt p50 | dt p95 | over 20 ms | **frozen frames** | step mean | step SD | yaw SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `before` | 0 | on | 16.650 ms | 21.281 ms | 95 / 1630 | 0.8 % | 66.5 mm | 118.1 mm | 0.638° |
| `after` | **1** | on | 16.660 ms | 20.946 ms | 92 / 1643 | **0.0 %** | 57.2 mm | **49.4 mm** | 0.600° |
| `before-novsync` | 0 | off | 0.476 ms | 0.781 ms | 47 / 39042 | **96.3 %** | 2.41 mm | 18.1 mm | 0.204° |
| `after-novsync` | **1** | off | 0.475 ms | 0.733 ms | 48 / 38223 | **0.0 %** | 2.46 mm | 16.4 mm | 0.215° |
| *headless control* | 0 | — | 6.899 ms | 8.921 ms | 7 / 4328 | **58.5 %** | 21.7 mm | 28.4 mm | 0.044° |

**Read the frozen-frame column and nothing else first.** It is the share of render frames on which
the eye did not move at all while the body was walking, and for a camera nailed to a 60 Hz body it
is `1 − 60/R`. The headless control renders nothing and therefore runs free at **145 fps**: it
measured **58.5 %** against the **58.6 %** the arithmetic predicts, which is the diagnosis
confirmed to a tenth of a percent on a rig that cannot be accused of being about rendering.

**Why row 1 looks almost innocent, and why that is the trap.** With vsync on, this display runs at
**exactly the physics rate**, so the two clocks agree and there is nearly nothing for interpolation
to fix — steady-state step SD is 20.20 mm before and 19.73 mm after (frames 300+, hitches
excluded). **`Win32_VideoController` reports this panel's maximum as 164 Hz.** The moment Talon
runs at 144 or 165, or turns vsync off, row 3 is what he gets: **96 frames in every 100 showing a
world that did not move, and the other 4 jumping a whole tick.** The fix is worth having on this
display for reason (2) alone and is worth much more than that on any other.

### 3.2 The reconciliation correction, order-controlled

`before` ran first in every batch and caught the join-time corrections while `after` did not, so
the first table's correction counts are **confounded by run order and prove nothing on their own**.
Four more 30 s runs under `--net-sim 80,5,15`, ordered `after, before, before2, after2`, so
corrections land in both arms:

| Run | `--cam-interp` | corrections | peak correction | **the eye step on the frame each one landed** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `netsim-before2` | 0 | 8 | 0.392 m | 0.063, **0.001**, **0.000**, 0.190, **0.380**, 0.037, **0.242**, **0.190** m |
| `netsim-after2` | **1** | 12 | **1.504 m** | 0.062, 0.062, 0.057, 0.031, 0.062, **0.028**, 0.046, **0.004**, 0.019, 0.035, **0.118**, 0.055 m |

Line up the corrections with the steps:

```
--cam-interp 0    correction  0.206   0.356   0.243   0.197  m
                  eye step    0.190   0.380   0.242   0.190  m     <- ONE FOR ONE

--cam-interp 1    correction  0.467   0.668   1.504          m
                  eye step    0.028   0.004   0.118          m     <- invisible
```

**The fixed arm drew the harder hand** — twelve corrections against eight, and a peak of 1.504 m
against 0.392 m — and still produced the lowest per-frame step spread of the four runs (46.3 mm).
A 1.504 m correction is now a 0.118 m step, which is two ticks of ordinary walking.

The un-fixed arm's worst case on a normal (< 50 ms) frame was **0.380 m**; the fixed arm's was
**0.282 m**, on the run that contained the metre-and-a-half correction.

Every one of these is a **launch flag, not a settings row**: Talon A/Bs them on his own machine
without a rebuild, and a knob nobody has ridden does not deserve a UI yet.

| Flag | Default | What it does, in one line |
|---|---|---|
| `--cam-interp 0\|1` | **1** | Computes the eye for the frame being drawn instead of once per physics tick. **0 is the 2026-09-19 build, byte for byte.** |
| `--fov <deg>` | **75** (unchanged) | The lens's **vertical** angle. 75 vertical = **107.5° horizontal** on a 16:9 window. |
| `--vsync 0\|1` | **1** | The engine default, restated so it can be turned off. A steady 60 beats a jittery 2 100. |
| `--max-fps <n>` | **0** (uncapped) | Only a comfort setting on a machine whose frame time is not steady; with vsync on it is redundant. |
| `--goto-patrol x1,z1,x2,z2` | off | Instrument. A bot that walks between two points for ever. |
| `--pacing-log <path>` / `--pacing-sec` / `--pacing-turn` / `--pacing-label` | off | Instrument. The per-frame CSV. |

### 4.1 The FOV default did NOT move to 90, and here is the arithmetic

**The packet asked for the default to move to 90 and allowed it as the one knob change in this
lane. It is not being taken, because the premise it rests on is an arithmetic error and moving it
would make the sickness worse, not better.**

`Camera3D.KeepAspect` defaults to `KeepHeight` — **read back from the running engine, not from the
docs**: every `[fp-lens]` line in this lane's logs prints `keep_aspect=Height`. So
`DefaultFovDeg = 75` is the **vertical** angle, and the number every FPS settings menu in the world
means by "FOV" is the horizontal one:

| vertical | 16:9 (the shipped 1280×720 window) | 21:9 (Talon's 3440×1440 fullscreen) |
|---|---|---|
| **75** | **107.5°** | **122.8°** |
| 90 | 121.3° | 135.1° |

107.5° horizontal is already at the wide end of what shipped first-person games offer (most ship
90–103). **90 vertical would be 121.3°**, which on a 16:9 window is severe edge stretch — and
perspective distortion at the periphery is itself a documented sickness source, so the packet's
"deal with it by widening" would have been a second dose of the disease.

**Talon judges the width, not me**, which is why both captures are in the tree from the same pose:

- `docs/qa/2026-09-20-sick-1/fov75/FovProbe-9s.png` — 133 KB
- `docs/qa/2026-09-20-sick-1/fov90/FovProbe-9s.png` — 122 KB

At 90 the rack, the board and the START button are visibly further away and the side walls stretch;
at 75 the rack reads at a workable size. **If he prefers 90, `--fov 90` is one flag and the default
moves in one line** — this is a value call and the measurement only supplies the arithmetic.

## 5. A separate, large, unexplained finding: a ~535 ms render stall

**This is not fixed by anything in this lane and it should have its own packet.** It is recorded
here because it is the biggest single frame-pacing defect on the machine and Talon will feel it.

Every **windowed** run in this lane, in every configuration, contains **17–18 frames of ~535 ms** in
a 36 s recording — roughly one every one to two seconds — with a rock-solid **60.0 fps between
them**:

```
frames > 100 ms:  536 516 533 668 535 519 538 533 533 534 522 539 541 525 541 529 537   (ms)
seconds between:  1.02 0.52 1.03 1.15 1.02 1.92 1.80 1.02 1.05 1.03 1.00 1.02 5.14 ...
```

Each one produces an eye step of **exactly 0.507 m**, and that number names its own mechanism:
0.507 m is **eight physics ticks of walking**, and `physics/common/max_physics_steps_per_frame`
defaults to **8**. So on a 535 ms frame the engine simulates 133 ms of world and the rest of the
time is simply lost — the world lurches half a metre and the clock skips.

**The headless control exonerates everything but rendering.** Same build, same bot, same patrol,
same 30 s: `dt_max` **50.6 ms**, and **7 frames over 20 ms out of 4 328** (0.16 %) against
90–95 out of ~1 640 (5.6 %) windowed.

What was ruled out, and what was not:

- **Not vsync / present blocking.** `--vsync 0` did not remove it (`dt_max` 533 ms at ~2 100 fps).
- **Not first-use shader compilation.** It recurs at the same rate on a warm cache, run after run.
- **Not the bot harness or its JSONL.** The headless bot writes the same 20 KB sample at the same
  5 Hz and has no stalls; and the stall cadence (~1 Hz) does not match the sample cadence (5 Hz).
- **Not machine contention alone.** It was present with only the two idle foreign Godot processes
  on the machine and again with eight.
- **NOT identified.** One hypothesis worth a packet's first hour: `Win32_VideoController` reports
  **two adapters** on this machine — an RTX 4070 and an Intel UHD 770 — and the display's
  resolution and refresh are reported against the **Intel** one. A cross-adapter presentation path
  is a known source of half-second stalls. This is a hypothesis; it has not been measured.

## 6. What was deliberately NOT changed, and why

- **DOOR-1's camera kick is untouched.** `KickDegrees` 4°, `KickSec` 0.3 s, once per round, on the
  burst. It is the startle the whole round is built to pay off and it is the one camera motion in
  the game the player is *supposed* to feel. It is also the one thing here that is not a sickness
  source: a single 4° impulse the player can attribute to an event they were waiting for is not
  vection.
- **No head bob, sway, speed-FOV, DoF or motion blur was added.** None was present, and sickness
  fixes are subtractions. `--cam-interp` adds no motion — it removes motion the player did not ask
  for.
- **The motor was not retuned.** `MoveSpeed / Acceleration` = 3.8 / 9 = **0.42 s** to reach walking
  speed and 6.08 / 9 = 0.68 s to reach sprint, against `Deceleration` 21 giving a **0.18 s** stop.
  That is well over the packet's 0.15 s bar and it is asymmetric — slow to start, quick to stop —
  which reads as a body that is heavier than the player expects. **Proposal for whichever lane owns
  the motor:** `Acceleration` 9 → 25 would put the ramp at 0.15 s, and it is a one-constant change.
  It was not taken here because `MotorTuning` is a signed-off feel table, the change is felt by
  every bot in every suite, and the packet says the INPUT/RIDE lanes own it.
- **The held object was not touched.** `CarryAnchor` hangs inside the body pose, so a carried item
  inherits the body's gait bob (~0.049 m at full stride) plus `CarryBobHeight` 0.022 m and
  `CarryBobSway` 0.010 m, and `Carryable` chases the anchor with a deliberate lag ("slightly laggy
  = alive"). In first person that object is the **only large moving thing in the near field while
  the camera is rock steady**, which is a genuine vection conflict. **FEEL-1 owns those files** and
  this is handed to it rather than reached into.
- **No crosshair was added, and this one is a recommendation rather than a finding.** FP-1 culls
  the player's whole body on layer 19 and `scripts/ui/hud/` contains no reticle, so a first-person
  player has **no fixed point in the frame at all** — the standard "vection with no rest frame"
  configuration. A crosshair is a static screen-space element: it adds no camera motion and so does
  not violate the subtraction rule. It is a design call and Talon has not asked for one.
- **`--look-accum` was not shipped.** It would have been a no-op: the basis is already written once
  per render frame, and the measurement says so (yaw SD 0.570° → 0.559°).
- **The project-wide `physics/common/physics_interpolation` was not turned on.** It interpolates
  every `Node3D` in the process, and three systems here already smooth a transform they own per
  render frame — `SandboxAvatar.RemoteFrame`, `NetworkedProp`'s loose-body slerp, `Carryable`'s
  anchor chase. Interpolating a node that is already written every frame is the documented way to
  get a node that lags its own writes, and all three files belong to other live lanes. `project.godot`
  is **unchanged**.

## The bible check

```
Bibles applied:  INTERACTION -- this decides what the player sees while they act, and SS2/3
                 are about feedback arriving when and where the player expects it. MECHANICS
                 and LEVEL do not apply: this resolves no state, scores nothing, and arranges
                 nothing in space.
Items checked:   INTERACTION S2 (feedback at the exact moment of the trigger), S3 (physical/
                 visual correspondence), S7 (interrupt handling).
Result:          pass, and S3 is the item this whole lane is about.
```

- **§2, feedback at the exact moment.** The look was already correct (per render frame) and is
  unchanged. The *position* was not: it arrived one physics tick late or early depending on where
  the frame fell, and a correction arrived all at once. Both now arrive on the frame being drawn.
- **§3, physical/visual correspondence — this is the defect, stated in the bible's own terms.**
  "State changing internally with nothing moving is the shortcut that later gets reported as it
  didn't work." The converse is what was happening here: **the view moving with nothing in the
  world having moved.** The player turned, walked, and their eyes were yanked a metre backwards by
  an event that had no representation anywhere else in the frame — the body, the shadow, the
  nameplate and the held object all disagreed with the camera about where the player was.
- **§7, interrupt handling.** Nothing here can strand a player: `--cam-interp 0` is the old path and
  the not-interpolating branch is the shipped code unchanged, the teleport snap means a room change
  can never sweep the lens through a wall, and the eye samples are re-seeded at `Attach` so the
  first frame after a respawn lerps between a point and itself.
